Defendants did not choose to adduce any evidence to establish that signature
of their father on Ex.A-1 is forged  apart from the plea of forgery, defendants counsel
contended that B. Siva Nagi Reddy who is the father of the plaintiffs did not complied
the prerequisite condition u/Sec. 16 (c) of Specific Relief Act by tendering the balance
of sale consideration to their father or to them or by depositing the amount in civil
court deposit account.  Sec. 16(c) of Specific Relief Act   reads as follows :
c) who fails to aver and prove that he has performed or has always
been ready and willing to perform the essential terms of the contract which
are to be performed by him, other than terms the performance of which has
been prevented or waived by the defendant.
Explanation: For the purposes of clause (c),-(i) where a contract
involves the payment of money, it is not essential for the plaintiff to
actually tender to the defendant or to deposit in court any money except
when so directed by the court; 
(ii) the plaintiff must aver performance of, or readiness and
willingness to perform, the contract according to its true construction.
21)
As seen from the recitals of Ex.A-1,  plaint schedule property handed over

 to the 1st plaintiff  i.e., B. Siva Nagi Reddy on the date of execution by H. Kale Saheb on
receipt of advance of sale consideration and agreed to  receive balance of sale
consideration as and when  B. Siva Nagi Reddy offers to him and registered the sale
deed without any litigation.  As seen from recitals of Ex.A-1, time is not the essence

Mohammed Ibrahim and another Vs. Mohammed Abdul Razzak   and  also referred
to para 28 and 29 wherein it is held that:
28.  The basic principle behind Section 16(c) of the Specific
Performance Act read with Explanation (ii) is that any person seeking
benefit of the specific performance of contract must manifest that his
conduct has been blemishless throughout entitling him to the specific relief
and the Court is to grant relief on the basis of the conduct of the person
seeking relief and that the meaning of such an averment of readiness and
willingness must be that the non-completion of the contract was not the
fault of the plaintiffs and that they were disposed and able to complete it
had it not been renounced by the defendant.  We do not intend to repeat
the decisions relied for the same proposition of law. (Vide Sugani V.
Rameshwar Das and another, 2006(5) ALT 10 (SC)) .
29. When the vendee was never ready and willing to perform his part
of contract, evident from his own conduct revealed from the registered
notices exchanged, we have no hesitation in stating that he was never ready
and willing to perform his part of contract and as such he was not entitled
to the relief of specific performance.

22)
The defendants projected that there is no compliance of Section 16(c) of
Specific Relief Act but the ruling submitted by the defendants is not applicable as to
the facts of the case.  On one hand, defendants denied the execution of Ex.A-1 by
their father by taking plea of forgery and on the other hand, they say that there is no
compliance of Sec. 16(c) of Specific Relief Act when the defendants takes the plea of
forgery they cannot go with the plea that there is no compliance of mandatory
provision of Section 16(c) of Specific Relief Act.  Further, the defendants did not
adduce any documentary  evidence to show that they are in possession and enjoyment
of the plaint schedule property.  The defendants got marked Ex.B-1 in order to
establish that their father died on 29-10-2004 and Ex.B-2 also filed i.e., the wedding
card of Zubeda Bee who is the daughter of  late H. Kale Saheb by pointing these
documents defendants counsel argued that the said person was not brought on
record and suit is not maintainable as there are no proper parties.  But here in the
present case there is no any evidence to show that the said Zubeda Bee is having
interest over the plaint schedule property.  Hence, the plea of non joinder of parties is
ignored.  Hence, the issue is settled in favour of the plaintiffs and plaintiffs are
entitled to relief of Specific Relief Act against the defendants.

the defendants to execute the registered sale deed as they did not given any reply to
the legal notice.  The counsel for the plaintiffs submitted citation reported on 29
October 2014 of Hon’ble Supreme Court of India Civil Appeal Nos.  9949-9950 of
2014  in between Rathnavathi and Another Vs. Kavita Ganashamdas  and relied
upon para Nos.  49 and 50 wherein it is held that :
49. Mere  reading of Article 54 of the Limitation Act would show that
if the date is fixed for performance of the agreement, then non-compliance
of the agreement on the date would give a cause of action to file suit for
specific  performance within three years from the date so fixed.  However,
when no such date is fixed, limitation of three years to file a suit  for specific
performance  would begin when the plaintiff has noticed that the
defendant has refused the performance of the agreement.
50.  The case at hand admittedly does not fall in the first category of
Article 54 of the Limitation Act because as observed supra, no date was
fixed in the agreement for its performance.  The case would thus be
governed by the second category viz., when plaintiff has a notice that
performance is refused.
explained that time bound is taken when plaintiffs noticed refusal on the part of the
obligants.  Admittedly defendants have knowledge of Ex.A-1 i.e., executed by their
father Exs. A-3 and A-4 shows the same where the defendants did not gave any reply.
Hence, the suit is not barred by limitation.   Accordingly, additional issue is answered in
favour of the plaintiffs and against the defendants.
