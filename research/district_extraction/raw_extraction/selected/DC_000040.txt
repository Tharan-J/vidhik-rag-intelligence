09-2024. The facts of said case and present case are entirely different but
the guidelines given in considering the discharge application is made
applicable to this case. The Hon’ble High Court in supra has held that in
order to discharge an accused the Judge after considering the material,
should find that there is no sufficient ground for proceeding against
accused. The necessary corollary is that if the Judge finds sufficient
grounds for proceeding against the accused, discharge cannot be
considered and plea of discharge must fail. Thus crucial aspect to be
considered when considering discharge U/Sec. 227 of Cr.P.C as well as
U/Sec. 250 (2) of BNSS indubitably is nothing but as to whether sufficient
ground to proceed against the accused is made out from prosecution
records. If sufficient materials are available to go for trial, discharge cannot
be considered. In the said case the Hon’ble Court has rejected the revision
by contending that there is prima-facie case in the said case. In present
case on hand there is prima-facie case such as panchanma, photo,
property record, 180 statement and seizure panchanma .  
38.
Therefore at this stage, the information and also the materials
available in the case including statements recorded U/Sec. 180 of BNSS,
wound certificate and seizure panchanma do discloses prima-facie
cognizable offence against the accused persons and there is nothing to
come to conclusion that the allegations are inherently improbable or being
maliciously instituted at this stage. Thus, Point No.1 is answered in
“Negative”.
