10.
Now, I would like to discuss the application of
law for offence under Section 138 of N.I. Act.
11.
Section 139 of the N.I. Act provides that unless
contrary is proved, it shall be presumed that
the holder of the cheque received the cheque
for the discharge of any debt or other liability.
12.
The Hon'ble Supreme Court in Hiten P. Dalal
Vs. Bratindranth Banerjee, reported in
(2001) 6 SCC 16, has held that once the
signature is admitted by the accused then the
presumption under Section 118(a) and Section
139 of the Act come into operation and it is
presumed that the cheque in question was
issued for discharge of legally enforceable debt
or liability.
13.
The scope of presumption under Section

118(a) and Section 139 of N.I. Act was
summarized by the Hon'ble Supreme Court in
the case of Basalingappa Vs. Mudibasappa,
reported in (2019) 5 SCC 418, as under :
"25.1. Once the execution of cheque is
admitted Section 139 of the Act mandates a
presumption that the cheque was for the
discharge of any debt or other liability.
25.2. The presumption under Section 139
is a rebuttable presumption and the onus is on
the accused to raise the probable defence. The
standard of proof for rebutting the presumption
is that of preponderance of probabilities.
25.3. To rebut the presumption, it is open
for the accused to rely on evidence led by him
or the accused can also rely on the materials
submitted by the complainant in order to raise
a

probable

defence.

Inference

of
preponderance of probabilities can be drawn
not only from the materials brought on record
by the parties but also by reference to the
circumstances upon which they rely.
25.4. That it is not necessary for the
accused to come in the witness box in support
of his defence, Section 139 imposed an
evidentiary burden and not a persuasive
burden.

25.5. It is not necessary for the accused
to come in the witness box to support his
defence."
14.
The legal position which emerges from the
aforesaid judgments is that once it is admitted
or proved that the accused had issued the
cheque or proved that the signature on the
cheque is of the accused, it is presumed (u/s
118 and u/s 139 of N.I. Act) that the cheque in
question had been made or drawn for
consideration and that the holder of the
cheque received the cheque for discharge of a
debt or liability.
However, the said presumptions under
Section 118 and 139 of the Act are rebuttable
in nature. The accused may rebut these
presumptions by leading cogent defence
evidence or from the case set out by the
complainant.
15.
Now reverting back to the case in hand it is
seen from the evidence on record that the
accused-appellant could not prove that he had
not received any notice from the respondentcomplainant. It is also seen that demand notice
was also received within the stipulated period
of 30 days. Moreover, the cheque vide
No.000004 dated 06.09.2023 bears the
