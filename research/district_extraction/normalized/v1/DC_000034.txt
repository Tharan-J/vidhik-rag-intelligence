8
consideration and did not come forward to pay the same to the defendants even by
way of money order. Sec. 16 (c) of Specific Relief Act reads as follows :
c) who fails to aver and prove that he has performed or has always
been ready and willing to perform the essential terms of the contract which
are to be performed by him, other than terms the performance of which has
been prevented or waived by the defendant.
Explanation: For the purposes of clause (c),-(i) where a contract
involves the payment of money, it is not essential for the plaintif to
actually tender to the defendant or to deposit in court any money except
when so directed by the court;
(ii) the plaintif must aver performance of, or readiness and
willingness to perform, the contract according to its true construction.
22)
As seen from the recitals of Ex.A-1, plaint schedule property handed over to
the plaintif at the time of receiving the advance of sale consideration by the father of
the defendants late H. Kale Saheb. Further, it is also averred that when the plaintif
intends to pay the balance of sale consideration, defendants father agreed to receive
the same and executed registered sale deed under Ex.A-1. As seen from Ex.A-2, he
issued specific legal notice by expressing his intention to ofer the balance of sale
consideration and called defendants to execute the registered sale deed.
23)
As seen from Ex.A-1 no time is fixed for execution of the registered sale deed
then time is not the essence of contract that too there is no evidence to show that
defendants were still in possession of the plaint schedule property. The counsel for
the defendants relied upon the decision reported in 2007(2) L.S. 146 (D.B.) in

--- PAGE 9 ---
9
between Mohammed Ibrahim and another Vs. Mohammed Abdul Razzak and also
referred to para 28 and 29 wherein it is held that:
28. The basic principle behind Section 16(c) of the Specific
Performance Act read with Explanation (ii) is that any person seeking
benefit of the specific performance of contract must manifest that his
conduct has been blemishless throughout entitling him to the specific relief
and the Court is to grant relief on the basis of the conduct of the person
seeking relief and that the meaning of such an averment of readiness and
willingness must be that the non-completion of the contract was not the
fault of the plaintifs and that they were disposed and able to complete it
had it not been renounced by the defendant. We do not intend to repeat
the decisions relied for the same proposition of law. (Vide Sugani V.
Rameshwar Das and another, 2006(5) ALT 10 (SC)) .
29. When the vendee was never ready and willing to perform his part
of contract, evident from his own conduct revealed from the registered
notices exchanged, we have no hesitation in stating that he was never ready
and willing to perform his part of contract and as such he was not entitled
to the relief of specific performance.

ADDITIONAL ISSUE NO.1 :- The main contention of the defendants is that the
suit is barred by limitation. The counsel for the defendants argued that from the date
of refusal, performance of part of contract on defendants side has to be taken into
consideration during the life time of late H. Kale Saheb by pointing them. The
plaintif admitted in his pleadings of plaint that there is an oral demand made by the
plaintif to late H. Kale Saheb and counsel for the defendants pointed out the
admission of P.W-1 that suit is filed after the death of H. Kale Saheb i.e., after death of
H. Kale Saheb and filed death certificate of H. Kale Saheb. Further also pointed that
plaintif noticed refusal of amounts and registration and no notice issued. The counsel
for the plaintif argued that Ex.A-1 is not fixed that the time bond and after issuing
Ex.A-2 it came to know that the defendants refused to perform their part of contract
and submitted a case law and relied upon the decision reported on 29 October 2014
of Hon'ble Supreme Court of India Civil Appeal Nos. 9949-9950 of 2014 in between
Rathnavathi and Another Vs. Kavita Ganashamdas and relied upon para Nos. 49
and 50 wherein it is held that :
49. Mere reading of Article 54 of the Limitation Act would show that if the
date is fixed for performance of the agreement, then non-compliance of the
agreement on the date would give a cause of action to file suit for

--- PAGE 11 ---
11
specific performance within three years from the date so fixed. However,
when no such date is fixed, limitation of three years to file a suit for specific
performance would begin when the plaintif has noticed that the
defendant has refused the performance of the agreement.
50. The case at hand admittedly does not fall in the first category of
Article 54 of the Limitation Act because as observed supra, no date was
fixed in the agreement for its performance. The case would thus be
governed by the second category viz., when plaintif has a notice that
performance is refused.
26)
 As per the rulings submitted by the plaintif, the time bound to be taken when
the plaintif noticed refusal on the part of obligant admittedly defendants have
knowledge of execution of Ex.A-1 by their father prior to the issue of legal notice,
defendants received legal notice, it is evident from the acknowledgment cards i.e.,
Exs. A-2 to A-4. Hence, the suit is not barred by limitation. Accordingly, additional
issue is answered in favour of the plaintif and against the defendants.
