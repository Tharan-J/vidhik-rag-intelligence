ORDER 
  
R. DEVDAS J., (ORAL): 
 
 
The prayer in this writ petition is to direct the 
5th respondent-Special Land Acquisition Officer to 
pay 
the 
difference 
amount 
of 
compensation.  
Although it is mentioned in the prayer that the 
difference amount of compensation as per the new 
Act, 2013 may be paid, the Learned Counsel for the 
petitioner submits that the said prayer is not correct 
therefore, he does not press that portion of the 
prayer regarding new Act, 2013. However, the 
learned Counsel for the petitioner admits that the 
compensation was determined in favour of the 
petitioner, although her name was not reflected in 
the RTC. The name of the Government was 
reflected in the RTC but, taking into consideration 
the admitted facts that the petitioner and similarly 
placed persons were in occupation of certain 
portions of the lands and subsequently, their names 
were mutated in the revenue records by the 


--- PAGE 4 ---
 
- 4 - 
 
 
Assistant 
Commissioner 
and 
the 
award 
was 
determined in accordance with law.  It is not 
disputed that on 13.02.2019, 15.02.2019 and 
18.02.2019, in all a total sum of Rs.1,80,05,000/- 
was received by the petitioner as compensation 
under Section 29(2) of the Act, on the petitioner 
entering into an agreement and on furnishing an 
indemnity bond.  
 
 2.  In  that   view    of      the   matter, 
learned 
Counsel 
for 
respondent-KIADB, 
Sri.P.V.Chandrashekar submits that this Court has 
decided 
earlier 
that 
in 
such 
matters 
where 
compensation has been received by the petitioner 
after having entering into an agreement under 
Section 29(2) of the Act, it is not permissible for 
such parties to seek enhanced compensation. 
Learned Counsel draws the attention of this Court 
to a decision in the case of Sri.Mallikarjun Vs. 
Special Land Acquisition Officer and Another, in 
W.P.No.11095/2021, 
decided 
11.08.2021. 
This 


--- PAGE 5 ---
 
- 5 - 
 
 
Court has noticed the decision of the Hon’ble Apex 
Court in the case of Ishwarlal Premchand Shah  
Vs.  State of Gujarat, (1996) 4 SCC 174 and 
Desigowda 
Vs. 
 
Karnataka 
Industrial 
Area 
Development Board, ILR 1995 KAR 2250 and held 
that 
the 
petitioner 
having 
entered 
into 
an 
agreement with the respondent authorities in terms 
of Section 29(2) of the Act cannot be permitted to 
resile from the agreed compensation amount and 
their claim for reconsideration also cannot be 
accepted in view of the law laid down by the 
Hon’bel Supreme Court.  
 
3. Therefore, for the reasons stated in the 
decision in the case of Sri.Mallikarjun (supra), the 
writ petition stands dismissed.  
 
4. In view of disposal of the main petition, 
I.A.No.1/2021 does not survive for consideration 
and is accordingly disposed of.
