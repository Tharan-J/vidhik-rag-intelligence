ORDER 
 
 
 
This Revision Petition is filed by the husband 
challenging the Order dated 20.04.2019 passed by the III 
Additional Principal Family Judge at Mysuru (henceforth 
referred to as 'Trial Court') in C.Mis.No.352/2017 by which 
it directed the petitioner to pay a monthly maintenance of 
`6,000/- to the respondent herein. 
 
2. 
The respondent filed a petition under Section 
125 Cr.P.C. claiming maintenance of `25,000/- per month. 
The respondent claimed that she was given in marriage to 
the petitioner on 02.05.2012 at Mysuru and they led a 
blissful married life for a year. However, thereafter the 
petitioner and his parents harassed and tortured her 
mentally to bring dowry from her parents' house or to 
divorce 
the 
petitioners. 
She 
claimed 
that 
though 
panchayats were conveyed, the petitioner and his parents 
did not turn up. The respondent alleged that she was 
driven out of the matrimonial home during October-2016, 


--- PAGE 3 ---
 
 
3 
 
 
  
which forced her to file a petition for dissolution of 
marriage in M.C. No. 621/2016 before the Additional 
Family Court, Mysuru which is pending consideration. The 
respondent claimed that the petitioner did not make any 
efforts to accept her back into the matrimonial fold. 
Therefore, she was neglected by the petitioner and since 
she had no source of income, she was entitled to a 
monthly maintenance of a sum of `25,000/- as the 
petitioner was running catering business and was earning 
`50,000/- per month. She alleged that he was also a 
money lender and possessed landed properties and was 
also earning rental income at Hinkal.  
 
3. 
The petitioner contested the petition and 
claimed that he was an agriculturist and he was earning a 
sum of `20,000 per annum. He alleged that it was the 
petitioner who was cruel and responsible for marring the 
marriage. 
 
4. 
Based on these rival contentions, the case was 
set down for trial. The respondent was examined as PW1 


--- PAGE 4 ---
 
 
4 
 
 
  
and she marked documents as Ex.P1 and P2. The 
petitioner was examined as RW1 and he marked 
documents as Exs.R1 to R10.  
 
5. 
In the light of the relationship that was 
admitted by the parties, the Trial Court held that due to 
irreconcilable 
differences, 
the 
parties 
were 
residing 
separately. In so far as the claim for maintenance is 
concerned, the Trial Court held that the petitioner 
possessed two acres of land in Sy.No.103/4, 1 acre 20 
guntas in Sy.No.104/3, 1 acre each in Sy.Nos.104/1 and 
104/4. Therefore, the Trial Court held that the petitioner 
had sufficient means from the said properties. The Trial 
Court noticed the inconsistent statement of the petitioner 
that on one breath he claimed that he suffered loss in 
business but on the other, he claimed that he was not 
doing any business. Therefore, the Trial Court held that the 
contention of the respondent that the petitioner was doing 
catering business was probable. The Trial Court noticed 
that the respondent did not have any source of income and 


--- PAGE 5 ---
 
 
5 
 
 
  
therefore, passed an order directing the petitioner to pay a 
sum of `6,000/- per month as maintenance and a sum of 
`5,000/- as litigation expenses. 
 
6. 
Being aggrieved by the aforesaid order, the 
present petition is filed. 
 
7. 
The 
learned 
counsel 
for 
the 
petitioner 
submitted that there was no proof to establish that the 
petitioner was earning any income from catering business. 
He contended that the respondent owned 2 acres 15 
guntas of land where she was growing paddy, ginger and 
banana and earning sufficiently well. He claimed that 
respondent had not disclosed her holding of land, she was 
not entitled to any maintenance.  
 
8. 
Per contra, the learned counsel for the 
respondent submitted that the petitioner possessed 
agricultural 
lands 
and 
he 
had 
not 
disclosed 
the          
income generated from the said property. He also 
contended that the petitioner did not disclose the nature of 


--- PAGE 6 ---
 
 
6 
 
 
  
business that he had conducted and the nature of loss that 
he had suffered. Therefore, he contended that the 
petitioner was not truthful in his defence before the Trial 
Court. He claimed that the respondent did not own any 
land as alleged. Having regard to the fact that the 
respondent did not have any means of livelihood, the Trial 
Court has rightly awarded maintenance of a sum of 
`6,000/- per month.  
 
9. 
I have considered the submissions made by 
the learned counsel for both the parties.  
 
10. 
The relationship between the parties is not in 
dispute. The parties have warred against each other by 
filing complaints before the police. The petitioner has 
sought for dissolution of the marriage. Therefore, there is 
enough reason for the respondent to stay away from the 
petitioner. Though the petitioner claimed in his objection 
that he did not possess any properties, but yet in the 
cross-examination he admitted that he possessed 4 acres 
20 guntas of land. Therefore, the petitioner was not 


--- PAGE 7 ---
 
 
7 
 
 
  
truthful in his case before the Trial Court. Though he 
claimed that he had suffered loss in the business, he did 
not disclose the nature of business that he had 
undertaken. On the contrary, he claimed that he was not 
doing any business. The petitioner did not place any 
material on record to show that the respondent had any 
source of income. 
 
11. 
In that view of the matter, the Trial Court was 
justified in awarding a sum of `6,000/- per month as 
maintenance having regard to the cost of living as well as 
the rate of inflation. There is no reason to interfere with 
the well considered Judgment of the Trial Court.  
 
Hence, this Revision Petition lacks merit and the 
same is dismissed.
