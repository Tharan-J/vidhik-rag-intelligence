This writ appeal is filed under Section 4 of Karnataka 
High Court Act, 1961, praying this Hon’ble Court to call for 
records in writ petition No.105865/2018 and set aside the 
order dated 05.11.2018 in W.P.No.105865/2018 and to allow 
the writ petition by allowing this writ appeal, in the interest of 
justice and equity.  
 
 
This appeal coming on for preliminary hearing, 
this day, Aravind Kumar J., delivered the following: 
 
JUDGMENT 
 
 This intra Court appeal is directed against the order 
dated 
05.11.2018, 
passed 
in 
W.P.No.105865/2018, 
whereunder writ petition filed by the appellant for issue of 
writ 
of 
certiorari 
namely 
to 
quash 
the 
order 
dated 
23.06.2018, passed by second respondent herein, referring to 
grant permission for conversion of lands from agricultural to 
non-agricultural purposes came to be rejected.  
 
2. 
Having heard learned Advocates appearing for the 
parties namely Shri Shivaraj P.Mudhol for appellant and 
Miss.Seema Shiva Naik, learned HCGP appearing for the State 
and on perusal of records, we have noticed that petitioner is 
the owner in possession and enjoyment of agricultural land 
bearing Sy.No.14/2, measuring 2 acres 1 gunta situated at 
Shaganamatti Village, Belagavi Taluka. He sought for 
conversion of said land from agricultural to non-agricultural 


--- PAGE 3 ---
 
 
 
 
 
: 3 : 
 
use (residential purpose)  by filing an application on 
23.10.2017 before the second respondent. Third respondent 
conducted spot inspection and verified the records submitted 
by petitioner along with his application and forwarded a 
report to second respondent recommending to accord 
permission. However, second respondent on receipt of report 
rejected the request of the petitioner on the ground that 
fourth respondent has intimated that lands situated in 
Shaganmatti village has been included in the proposed future 
developmental activities of Belagavi and a plan has been 
prepared as per raised Scheme-III by fourth respondent 
authority in its board meeting and it has been resolved to 
take up developmental activities in these lands.  
3. 
Rival contentions raised came to be considered by 
the learned Single judge and by order under challenge writ 
petition came to be rejected on the ground that petitioner is 
having alternate remedy of approaching the appellate 
authority under Section 49 of Karnataka Land Revenue Act 
and also that planning Section of the fourth respondent has 
already planned under Scheme-III for development of the 
land in question in its future endeavors.

4.

Shri

before the learned Single Judge and prays for allowing the 
appeal.  
 
5. 
Per contra,   Miss.Seema Shiva Naik, learned 
HCGP appearing for respondent would support the impugned 
order and prays for dismissal of the appeal.  
 
6. 
Insofar as contention raised with regard to 
maintainability of the writ petition is concerned, this Court is 
conscious of the fact that there is an alternate remedy 
available under Section 49 of the Act, whereunder petitioner 
can file an appeal challenging the order dated 23.06.2018 
(Annexure-E). Referring to entertain petition under Article 
226 
is 
a 
self 
imposed 
judicial 
restraint. 
Under 
all 
circumstances to come, parties would not be relegated to 
avail the alternate remedy. The exercise of writ jurisdiction 
under Article 226 of the Constitution of India for issuing 
prerogative writs would depend or myriad circumstances that 
may unfold. Circumstances warranting issuance of such writ 
would be examined on case to case basis. In the instant case, 


--- PAGE 5 ---
 
 
 
 
 
: 5 : 
 
it is noticed that under similar circumstances, coordinate 
benches of this Court as well as learned Single Judges have 
allowed similar pleas put forward by the writ petitioners 
therein, who were similarly placed as that of petitioner and 
having granted relief by arriving at a conclusion that such 
orders are passed under error of jurisdiction. Hence, we are 
of the considered view that it would not be apt and 
appropriate to direct the petitioner to avail the alternate 
remedy when the relief has been granted to similarly placed 
persons by the coordinate benches. In that view of the 
matter, contention raised in that regard cannot be accepted 
and it stands rejected. In other words, the finding recorded 
by the learned Single Judge in this regard would require to be 
set aside.  
 
7. 
Insofar as second contention relating to dismissal 
of writ petition on the ground that planning authority namely 
fourth respondent has already planned with regard to certain 
developmental activities had been proposed to be taken 
under Scheme-III in the village where land of the petitioner is 
situated, requires to be considered for purposes of out-right 
rejection. Said proposal is only at the initial stage, namely

resolution has been passed by fourth respondent in that 
regard and there is no substantial development of the land in 
question being sought to be used by issuance of appropriate 
preliminary notification. There is every possibility of the 
proposal being shelved also. In such circumstances, the 
owner of the land or immovable property cannot be prevented 
from filing an application to seek for conversion of his land, if 
he is otherwise entitled to do so. In fact, under similar 
circumstances, 
coordinate 
bench 
in 
the 
matter 
of 
Mohammed Faizuddin and another vs. the Secretary to 
the 
Government 
of 
Karnataka 
 
and 
others 
in 
W.A.Nos.200561/2016 & 200013/2017, disposed of 
dated 1st February 2017 has held that declining to grant 
permission, the prayer of petitioner on the ground that the 
State is proposing to set up National Investment and 
Manufacturing Zone  and the process has been initiated in 
that regard and in principle approval having been given by 
the Ministry of Commerce and Industries, Government of 
India would not be a ground, has held: 
 
“However, 
when 
the 
process 
relating 
to 
reserving the land for a particular purpose has not 
blossomed itself into decision having been taken or

: 7 : 
 
State having taken steps to acquire land for the said 
purpose as on the date writ petitioner submitted his 
application, right of petitioner to seek for conversion of 
land cannot be scuttled or it cannot be gainsaid by the 
State that petitioner does not have any such right to 
such conversion of the lands. In fact petitioner has 
submitted his application in the instant case on 
18.04.2014 with all relevant documents and No-
Objection Certificates obtained from the concerned 
departments 
and 
said 
rights 
to 
claim 
deemed 
conversion accrued to the petitioner immediately after 
four months period was over and as such respondent 
by the impugned endorsement could not have rejected 
the request of the petitioner on the ground that State 
Government 
is 
proposing 
to 
set 
up 
National 
Investment and Manufacturing Zone. In fact a right 
had accrued to the petitioner immediately after 
completion of four months i.e., 18.08.2014 in view of 
the deeming provision i.e., Section 95(5). Hence, 
impugned endorsement cannot be sustained.” 
 
8. 
Petitioner being similarly placed namely an 
application having been filed for conversion of his land 
bearing Sy.No.14/2b measuring 2 acres 1 gunta by 
submitting his application on 23.10.2017 was entitled for such 
relief. Hence, finding recorded by the learned Single Judge 
cannot stand the test of law as noticed by us herein above. In 
other words appellant is entitled to the relief as sought for.
