This Writ Petition is filed under Articles 226 and 227 of the 
Constitution of India praying to declare holding that the petitioners 
are entitled for restoration of the lands bearing Sy.No.27/2 (old 
Sy.No.27) measuring and extent 1 acres 31.08 guntas in view of 
Section 3(2)(a) and (b) of the Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) 
Repeal Act, 1999 and etc.  
 
This Writ Petition coming on for orders this day, the court 
made the following: 
 


--- PAGE 3 ---
 
 
3 
ORDER 
R. DEVDAS J., (ORAL): 
 
 
The petitioners are the children of late Sri. Venkataramaiah, 
who was re-granted occupancy rights in respect of Sy.Nos.27/2 and 
35/2, situated at Devarachikkanahalli Village, Bengaluru South 
Taluk, as seen in the order dated 28.09.1997 at Annexure-C, 
passed by the Assistant Commissioner, Bengaluru Sub-Division.  
 
 
2. 
This writ petition is confined to Sy.No.27/2 of 
Devarachikkanahalli Village, Bengaluru South Taluk.  
 
 
3. 
Learned counsel for the petitioners submits that while 
the order of re-grant was made in Sy.No.27/2, there was in all 3 
acres. However, after partition in the family, an extent of 1 acre 
31.08 guntas is said to have fallen into the share of the petitioners, 
who claim under late Sri. Venkataramaiah.  
 
 
4. 
It is an undisputed fact that the said lands were notified 
for acquisition for the formation of BTM Layout, IV Stage. However,  
series of writ petitions were filed before this Court seeking a 
declaration that the scheme insofar as certain lands stood lapsed.  


--- PAGE 4 ---
 
 
4 
 
5. 
The writ petition filed by the petitioners herein in  
W.P.Nos.41386-395/2017 was allowed by order dated 24.09.2019, 
following the decision of this Court in W.P.No.804/2016 and 
W.P.No.21831/2016, 
declaring 
that 
final 
notification 
dated 
03.11.1990 
issued 
under 
Section 
19(1) 
of 
the 
Bengaluru 
Development Authority Act, 1976 stood lapsed in respect of 
Sy.No.27/2 of Devarachikkanahalli Village, Begur Hobli, Bengaluru 
South Taluk to an extent 3 acres 23 guntas, including 2 guntas of 
kharab 
land. 
The 
petitioners 
approached 
the 
Assistant 
Commissioner seeking deletion of the name of the State 
Government entered in the revenue records and to substitute the 
name of the petitioners in view of the declaration made by this 
Court.  
 
 
6. 
Learned counsel for the petitioners submits that during 
the course of the proceedings before the Assistant Commissioner, it 
was brought to the notice of the petitioners herein that, insofar as 
the lands re-granted in favour of Sri. Venkataramaiah, proceedings 
were initiated under the provisions of the Urban Land Ceiling Act, 


--- PAGE 5 ---
 
 
5 
and in terms of the proceedings in MR.No.2/1997-98, the name of 
‘Government’ to extent of 7891.34 sq.mtr, has been entered.  
 
 
7. 
In the light of the said fact, the Assistant Commissioner 
held that he has no Authority under the Urban Land (Ceiling and 
Regulation) (Repeal) Act, 1999 and therefore he cannot entertain 
the claim under the provisions of the Karnataka Land Revenue Act. 
With 
these 
observations, 
the 
appeal 
insofar 
as 
late  
Sri. Venkataramaiah is concerned, was dismissed. Consequently, 
the writ petition has been filed.  
 
8. 
Learned counsel for the petitioners placing reliance on a 
decision of the Division Bench of this Court in the case of 
SMT.MALLAMMA 
VS. 
THE 
STATE 
OF 
KARNATAKA 
REVENUE 
DEPARTMENT AND ANOTHER reported in ILR 2008 KAR 4041,  
submits that the Hon’ble Division Bench has noticed the provisions 
under Section 3 of the Repeal Act, 1999 and has held that Section 
3(2) of the Act provides that where any land is deemed to have 
vested in the State Government under Sub-Section (3) of Section 
10 of the Principal Act but possession of which has not been taken 
over by State Government or any person duly authorized by the 


--- PAGE 6 ---
 
 
6 
State Government in this behalf or by the Competent Authority, and 
any amount has been paid by the State Government with respect to 
such land, then, such land shall not be restored unless the amount 
paid, is refunded to the State Government.  
 
9. 
It is submitted that the Hon’ble Division Bench found in 
that case, that the Revenue entries would show that possession was 
taken, however, it was also noticed that there was no determination 
of compensation and therefore, compensation was not paid to the 
petitioners therein. While noticing these factual aspects, the Hon’ble 
Division Bench allowed the Writ Appeal, set aside the order passed 
by the learned Single Judge and directed the Revenue Authorities to 
remove the name of ‘Government’ from the Revenue Records and 
substitute the name of the ‘land owner’. Learned counsel therefore 
submits that the matter stands squarely covered by the above said 
decision.  
 
10. 
Per contra, learned HCGP draws the attention of this 
Court to a communication dated 27.03.2002, which  is in the nature 
of clarification regarding procedure to be followed in certain cases 
relating to ULC matters. The said communication has been issued 


--- PAGE 7 ---
 
 
7 
by the Urban Development Department of the Government of 
Karnataka to all the Deputy Commissioners.  
 
11. 
Learned HCGP, while drawing the attention of this Court 
to paragraph No.2, of the said communication, submits that where 
the possession of the land has not been taken, the owner will get 
the benefit in consonance with the provisions of sub-section(2) of 
Section 3 of the Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) (Repeal) Act, 
1999. In such cases, the entries, if any, made in the RTC in favour 
of the Government will have to be rescinded. 
 
12. 
Learned HCGP therefore submits that as rightly held by 
the 
Assistant 
Commissioner, 
the 
petitioners 
should 
have 
approached the Deputy Commissioner under the provisions of the 
Repeal Act, 1999.  
 
13. 
Having heard the Learned counsel for the petitioners 
and the learned HCGP and on perusing the petition papers, this 
Court finds that the factual assertion made by the petitioners that 
the possession of the property in question was never taken away by 
the authorities acting under the Urban Land Ceiling Act and that 


--- PAGE 8 ---
 
 
8 
there was no determination of compensation are not contradicted 
by filing any objections.  
 
14. 
Nevertheless, as rightly submitted by the learned HCGP, 
the petitioners are required to approach the Deputy  Commissioner 
under the provisions of the Repeal Act, 1999. When  such an 
application is made before the Deputy Commissioner, the Deputy 
Commissioner has to look into the records, to find out as to 
whether possession was taken, whether compensation was 
determined, whether compensation was paid etc. After ascertaining 
the factual aspects, an order could be passed by the Deputy 
Commissioner.  
 
15. 
For the reasons stated above, liberty is granted to the 
petitioners to approach the Deputy Commissioner under the 
provisions of the Urban Land (Ceiling and Regulation) (Repeal) Act, 
1999 and if such an application is made, the Deputy Commissioner 
shall consider the application in the light of the observations made 
herein above and taking note of the communication dated 
27.03.2002, said to have been issued by the Department of Urban 


--- PAGE 9 ---
 
 
9 
Development, 
Government 
of 
Karnataka 
to 
the 
Deputy 
Commissioner.  
 
 
Consequently, the writ petition stands disposed of in the 
above terms.
