O R D E R
(Order of the Court was made by J.NISHA BANU, J.)
This habeas corpus petition has been filed by the detenu,
namely, Rasbudeen @ Rasthagir, S/o.Mohamathiar, aged about 28 years,
challenging the detention order No.94/2020 dated 28.12.2020, passed
by the second respondent, branding him as “Goonda” as contemplated
under Section 2(f) of Tamil Nadu Act 14 of 1982.
2.The learned counsel for the petitioner assails the order of
detention on the following grounds:-
(i) The detaining authority mechanically arrived the subjective
satisfaction relating to the possibility of the detenu for coming
out on bail in the ground case; (ii) There is an inordinate delay in
passing the detention order; (iii) The detaining authority pre-
judged the future activities of the detenue that if he comes out on
bail, the detenu will indulge in such future activities, which will
be prejudicial to the maintenance of public order.  But there is no
corresponding cogent materials in the booklet relating to the said
statement in the booklet; (iv) Several pages in the booklet are in
English and therefore, the detenu was not able to understand and
make effective representation; (vi)  The booklets are illegible and
therefore, the detenu cannot understand the said pages and prefer
effective representation and ; The Tamil and English version of the
grounds of detention and booklet differs and therefore, the detenu
was not able to effectively represent the case before the advisory
board.
3.Even though the petitioner has raised the above grounds to
quash the impugned detention order, the learned counsel for the
petitioner would mainly place arguments on the ground of delay in
disposal of the petitioner's representation.  In this regard, the
learned counsel for the petitioner would state that the procedural
safeguards guaranteed under Articles 21 and 22 of the Constitution
of India have not been followed in this case and there is
unexplained and inordinate delay in disposal of the petitioner's
representation which would vitiate the impugned order of detention.
Thus, he would pray to quash the impugned order of detention.
4.The learned Standing Counsel appearing for the respondents
would state that after satisfying with the materials placed by the
sponsoring authority, the detaining authority has passed the
impugned detention order and therefore, there is no infirmity or
illegality in the same.  He would produce the proforma regarding the
disposal of the petitioner's representation and would state that
even if there is any delay in disposal of the petitioner's
representation, it has not caused any prejudice to the rights of the
detenu.  Thus, he would pray for dismissal of this petition.

5.Heard the learned counsel for the petitioner as well as the
respondents.
6.Perusal of the proforma produced by the learned Standing
Counsel appearing for the respondents would show that as against the
impugned detention order, the petitioner has made a representation
to the 1st respondent on 04.01.2021, which was received on
05.02.2021.  Remarks on the said representation were called for on
05.02.2021 and it was received on 10.02.2021.  The Deputy Secretary
concerned has dealt with the representation on 10.02.2021 and the
Hon'ble Minister concerned has dealt with the representation on
12.04.2021 and finally, the representation came to be rejected on
15.04.2021.  It is seen that in between 10.02.2021 and 12.04.2021,
there is a delay of 60 days.  After excluding the government
holidays of 19 days, there is a delay of 41 days in considering the
petitioner's consideration.
7.At this juncture, it is useful to refer to the decision of
the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of Rajammal vs. State of Tamil
Nadu and another reported in 1999 (1) CC 417, wherein, the Apex
Court has held that it is for the authority concerned to explain the
delay, if any, in disposal of the representation of the detenu and
if any delay was caused on account of any indifference or lapse in
considering the representation, such delay will adversely affect
further detention of the prisoner.
8.In the case on hand, as stated supra, the delay of 41 days in
considering the representation of the petitioner remains unexplained
by the respondents.  Hence, in our considered view, the impugned
detention order is liable to be set aside solely on the ground of
delay by following the above decision of the Apex Court.
9.In fine, the Habeas Corpus Petition is allowed.  The
detention order No. 94/2020 dated 28.12.2020, passed by the second
respondent, is set aside.  Consequently, the detenu, namely,
Rasbudeen @ Rasthagir, S/o.Mohamathiar, aged about 28 years, who is
now detained at Madurai Central Prison, Madurai, is directed to be
released forthwith, unless his presence or custody or detention is
required in connection with any other case.
