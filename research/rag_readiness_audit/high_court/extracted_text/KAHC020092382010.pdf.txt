be stated in nutshell to the effect that suit property is 
ancestral property of defendant Nos.1 and 2.  Plaintiff has 
purchased the said property 20 years ago from the father 
of defendants.  Recently, plaintiff came to know that name

16.  Plaintiff claims to be in possession of the suit 
property since from the date of his purchase from father of 
defendant No.1.  However, on the death of father of 
defendants, their names have been recorded in the 
records of suit property.  In view of the said fact, 
panchayat was convened and defendants were alleged to 
have executed agreement of sale dated 20.02.2002 
Ex.P.1.  The recitals of agreement of sale Ex.P.1 does not 
speak anything about plaintiff being in possession of suit 
property, since he has purchased the same from the father 
of defendants.  However, the recitals of Ex.P.1 would go to 
show that plaintiff was put to be in possession of the suit 
property as on the date of agreement of sale dated 
20.02.2002.  It means that plaintiff was not in possession 
of the suit property about 20 years back, since he has 
purchased the same from the father of defendants.   
 
17. The evidence on record would demonstrate the fact 
that plaintiff was never put in possession of the suit 
property either when he claimed to have purchased from 
the father of defendants or under Ex.P.1.   The records of 
the suit property Ex.P.2 is recorded in the name defendant

18. The recitals of suit document agreement of sale

conveyance in terms of Article 5 (e)(i) of the Schedule of

19.  Indisputably, the suit property originally belongs to

the fact of Ex.P.1 agreement of sale in the suit filed by him

21. The Trial Court on the basis of comparison of 
signature of defendant No.1 on Ex.P.1 agreement of sale 
and denial of her signature on the vakalat of counsel 
Ex.P.3, recorded the finding that DW.1 suppressed the 
material fact and denied her signature on the vakalat 
Ex.P.3 held that defendants have received consideration 
amount of Rs.48,000/- under Ex.P.1.  On recording of

such finding, ordered for refund of consideration amount 
of Rs.48,000/- with interest.  The First Appellate Court 
also compared the signature of defendant No.1 on Exs.P.1 
and P.3 and held that the signature does not tally.  It is 
true that in terms of Section 73 of the Indian Evidence 
Act, 1872, empower the Court to compare the disputed 
and admitted signatures and record finding.  In the 
present case, defendant No.1 has not admitted her 
signature on Ex.P.1 as per Ex.P.1(b).  It is true that 
defendant No.1 has denied her signature on vakalat on 
Ex.P.3 as per Ex.P.3(a).  The said denial of defendant No.1 
of her own signature on the vakalat given to her counsel 
alone cannot be sufficient evidence to draw any inference 
that signature of defendant No.1 on Ex.P.1 is proved.  It is 
for the plaintiff who has approached the Court to prove the 
due execution of agreement of sale Ex.P.1 and cannot rely 
on the weakness of the defendants or denial of defendant 
No.1, signature on vakalat Ex.P.3.  The plaintiff cannot be 
allowed to take advantage of defendant No.1 denying her 
signature on the vakalat Ex.P.3 in proving the due 
execution of agreement of sale Ex.P.1.  Therefore, the
