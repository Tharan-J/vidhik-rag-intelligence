3.  The brief facts leading rise to filing of this appeal 
are as under: 
Plaintiff filed a suit for the relief of specific 
performance of contract in respect of the suit schedule 
property.  It is the case of the plaintiff that defendants 1 
to 3 are the absolute owners of the properties described in 
the schedule.  On 23.06.2006, defendant Nos.1 to 3 
executed an agreement of sale in favour of plaintiff 
agreeing to sell the schedule property to the plaintiff for a 
consideration of Rs.3,95,000/- to meet their urgent 
necessity and executed an agreement of sale in favour of 
the plaintiff on the same day.  On the execution of 
agreement of sale, defendants No.1 to 3 received a sum of 
Rs.25,000/- out of sale consideration of Rs.3,95,000/- as 
advance.  Defendants No.1 to 3 agreed to execute a 
registered sale deed within 5 months from the date of 
execution of an agreement and to receive the balance

executed a registered sale deed in favour of defendant 
No.4 on 07.07.2006.  Thereafter the plaintiff got issued

12.  The first appellate court has observed that the 
plaintiff has no financial capacity to purchase the suit 
property by paying the balance consideration amount.  
From the perusal of the written statement filed by 
defendant Nos.1 to 4, none of the defendants have denied 
about the financial capacity of the plaintiff in the written 
statement and further no suggestion was put to PW.1 that 
the plaintiff has no capacity to purchase the suit schedule 
property. 
 
 
13.  The appellate court has committed an error in 
recording a finding that the plaintiff has no financial 
capacity to purchase the suit land.  The appellate court 
except on that ground allowed the appeal and set aside 
the judgment and decree passed by the trial Court without 
considering the materials placed on record.  Hence, the

finding that the plaintiff has no financial capacity. The said 
finding is only on the basis of assumption and presumption 
without there being any material on record.  The appellate 
court has allowed the appeal solely on that ground that 
without going into the merits of the case. The appellate 
court without applying the judicial mind exercised the 
discretion under Section 20 of Specific Relief Act. The 
discretion exercised by the trial Court is not judicious .  
Hence, on these grounds itself, the impugned judgment is 
liable to be set aside.  In view of the above discussions, I 
answer the substantial question of law in the affirmative. 
And further the first appellate court has not considered the 
other aspect while deciding the appeal, as the matter 
requires reconsideration by the appellant Court. In view of 
the aforesaid facts and circumstances, I proceed to pass 
the following:
