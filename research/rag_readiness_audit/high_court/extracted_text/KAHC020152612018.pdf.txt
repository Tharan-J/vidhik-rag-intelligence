JUDGMENT 
 
The accused in Sessions Case No. 5007/2017 on the file 
of First Addl. Dist. & Sessions Judge, Uttara Kannada, (sitting 
at Sirsi) is the appellant, he has challenged his conviction for 
the offence u/s 302 IPC.  He has stood sentenced for life and 
fine of Rs.50,000/- with default imprisonment for a period of 
six months.  
 
2. 
The prosecution case in brief is that on 21.08.2016 
around 10.00 p.m. the accused set fire to his wife Nagaratna 
with an intention to kill her.  FIR was registered on 23.08.2016 
for the offences u/s 498-A, 504, 307 IPC.  Nagaratna died on 
09.11.2016 and then the offence u/s 302 IPC was included in 
the FIR.  The trial court charged the accused for the offences 
punishable u/s 498-A, 504 and 302 IPC.   
 
3. 
Out of 20 witnesses examined by the prosecution, the 
prominent witnesses PW1 to 4, 5, 10, 13 and 14 turned hostile, 
but the trial court took the view that inspite of hostility of these 
witnesses, the oral dying declaration leading to registration of 
FIR and another dying declaration recorded by PW16 and 17 
were believable and could be acted upon.   The defence theory

which finds support from the evidence given by hostile 
witnesses that the deceased caught fire accidentally while 
cooking is improbable to be believed in as much as she had 
suffered burn injuries on her face, lips, arms and fingers.  If the 
deceased had come in contact with fire while cooking, she 
should have suffered injuries on her lower limbs.  Since there 
were no injuries on the lower limbs, unless somebody had 
poured kerosene and set fire, the deceased would not have 
caught fire.  Drawing this conclusion, the trial court further held 
that the accused developed intention to kill his wife when the 
latter took objection to his drinking habit and returning home 
on the night of 21.08.2016 in a drunken state.  The accused 
was enraged and took resort to killing his wife.  Therefore an 
offence u/s 302 IPC was made out.  These are the findings of 
the trial court.

5. 
In the report made by PW1, the father of the deceased, 
to the Police on 23.08.2016 as per Ex.P1, it is stated that his 
son in law, i.e., the accused, was a driver and his daughter was 
working in a grocery store as a packer.  The matrimonial life of 
the deceased and the accused was good for four years after the 
marriage; the accused gradually became addicted to liquor; he 
started suspecting fidelity of his wife and for all these reasons 
there used to take place quarrels between them.  On 
21.08.2016, around 10 p.m. the deceased came running, fell 
down in front of the house of PW1 and hollered that her 
husband had burnt her.  She was consoled and taken to 
Government Hospital at Yallapur.  On the advice of the doctor 
at Yallapur, she was brought to KIMS Hospital, Hubli.  On 
23.08.2016, the deceased recovered to some extent and 
disclosed the reason for her being set on fire by the accused.  
Coming to know about the background, PW1 went to police 
station and gave a report to the police as per Ex.P.1 based on 
which FIR was registered.

10. 
In the case of Jayarajbhai (supra) the Hon’ble Supreme 
Court does not hold that dying declaration should not be acted 
upon, what is held is that the court has to weigh all the 
circumstances carefully and scrupulously before acting upon 
dying declaration, and ascertain that the declaration was 
voluntary, truthful and made in a conscious state of mind 
without being influenced by relatives or the investigating 
agency.  In effect what the Supreme Court has held is 
reiteration of the principles governing the evidence relating to 
dying declaration.   
 
11. 
In the case of Munnu Raja (supra), it is held:  
“6. 
The High Court has held that these statements are essentially 
true and do not suffer from any infirmity.  It is well settled that 
though a dying declaration must be approached with caution for the 
reason that the maker of the statement cannot be subjected to 
cross-examination, there is neither a rule of law nor a rule of 
prudence which has hardened into a rule of law that a dying 
declaration cannot be acted upon unless it is corroborated.  The High 
Court, it is strue, has held that the evidence of the two eyewitnesses 
corroborated the dying declarations but it did not come to the 
conclusion that the dying declarations suffered from any infirmity by 
reason of which it was necessary to look out for corroboration.” 
 
12. 
In the case of Sharief (supra), it is held that the dying 
declaration cannot be discarded on the ground that it was

law that dying declaration must necessarily be made to a 
Magistrate.  And on facts it was held by the Supreme Court that 
according to the opinion of the doctor the patient was conscious 
and her orientation was good to answer all the questions; 
therefore dying declaration cannot be discarded.   
 
13.

statements are consistent.    It is true that in Ex.P.14 there is 
an endorsement by the doctor, but that endorsement is not 
written in the way learned counsel for the appellant argued.  
The doctor did not make an endorsement that deceased was 
not in a condition to make statement. The actual endorsement

speak.  In fact, the said doctor who was examined as PW7 has

16. 
From the above discussion, we concur with the findings of 
the trial court that dying declaration can be acted upon, 
however it is difficult to hold that the entire evidence would 
lead to convicting the accused for the offence u/s 302 IPC.  It is 
to be noted here that though accused stood charged for 
offences u/s 498-A and 504 IPC, the trial court did not record 
conviction for these two offences on the premise that there was 
no evidence for the allegation that the accused was in the habit 
of harassing or intimidating his wife before the incident 
occurred on 21.08.2016.  What the evidence indicates is that 
when the deceased questioned the accused coming home in a 
drunken condition, he got irritated and picked up quarrel with 
his wife.  In that rage, it appears that he lost control over 
himself and resorted to throwing the things in the house helter-
skelter and then set fire to his wife by pouring kerosene.  This

act of accused does not indicate that the accused had intention 
to kill his wife, however it cannot be said that he had no 
knowledge of consequences.  Another important aspect to be 
mentioned here is that the deceased herself douzed the fire by 
pouring water on her, changed her dress and then ran towards 
her father’s house.  The evidence given by the doctors and also 
the postmortem report show the nature of burn injuries 
sustained by the deceased, definitely they were serious.  The 
death occurred on 09.11.2016.  It has come in the evidence of 
PW1 that the deceased herself insisted on her discharge from 
hospital at the time of Deepavali festival and thus she was 
taken to house against the doctor’s advice.  Chances of her 
survival was more had she not been discharged.  The 
postmortem report Ex.P.26 indicates that the cause of death 
was septicemia as a result of complications of burns sustained 
by deceased.  If the entire situation is analyzed, definitely it 
can be said that neither the offence u/s 302 nor u/s 304 IPC 
would get constituted.  We are of the opinion that the accused 
can be held guilty of Sec. 307 of IPC.  The ingredients of Sec. 
307 are presence of intention or knowledge about doing an act 
which might result in death.  We have already said that there

was no intention, however we cannot rule out the absence of 
knowledge on the part of the accused.  A prudent man is 
supposed to be aware of serious consequences ensuing when a 
person is set on fire.  In this view, disagreeing with the finding 
of the trial court in holding the accused guilty of the accused 
u/s 302 of IPC, we convict him for the offence u/s 307 IPC.   
 
17. 
As we have come to above conclusion, the accused 
cannot be punished for life imprisonment in the facts and 
circumstances even though Sec. 307 IPC provides for imposing 
life imprisonment.  The age of the accused at the time of 
incident appears to be around 30 years.  He has a son who still 
appears to be a minor.  In our opinion this is not a case for 
imposing life imprisonment.  He can be sentenced for a period 
of seven years with fine.  
 
18.
