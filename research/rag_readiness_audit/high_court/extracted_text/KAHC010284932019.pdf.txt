ORAL JUDGMENT 
This appeal is filed by the claimant challenging the 
judgment and award dated 16th April 2019, passed by the  I 
Additional Senior Civil Judge and Chief Judicial Magistrate, 
Mandya, (for short `Tribunal'),  in MVC No.1397/2017, seeking 
enhancement of compensation.  
2. 
For the sake of convenience, the parties are 
referred to as per their ranking before the Tribunal.  
3. 
Brief facts of the case are that, on 02.08.2017, at 
around 2.30 p.m., the claimant was traveling as a pillion rider 
on a motorcycle bearing registration No.KA-11/ED-9085 and his 
friend Sri. Ravishankar was riding the said vehicle. Both were 
going towards Bengaluru on Mysuru-Bengaluru road. When 
they were in front of Government Boys Collage Mandya, the 
driver of the offending car bearing registration No.KA-02/N-

referred to as the ‘offending vehicle’) contended that the 
accident occurred due to the negligence of the rider of the 
motorcycle. It denied the other averments made in the petition 
and prayed for dismissal of the petition. 
6. 
From the rival contentions of the parties, the 
Tribunal framed necessary issues. 
7.

to P-30. He also examined the doctor as CW-1 and marked 
Exs.C-1 to C-4. Respondents have not led any oral and

9. 
Being aggrieved by the said award passed by the 
Tribunal, the claimant has filed the present appeal for 
enhancement of compensation. 
10. 
Heard the arguments of the learned counsel for the 
appellant as well as respondent No.2.   
11. 
Undisputedly, the owner and insurer of the 
motorcycle were not parties before the Tribunal. In the absence

of necessary parties, the Tribunal considered negligence of 
rider of the motorcycle. However, in view of the law laid down 
by the Hon'ble Apex Court in the case of Khenyei Vs. New 
India Assurance Company Limited and Others1, in the 
absence of necessary parties, the Court cannot assess the 
negligence of driver of the vehicle involved in the accident. 
Moreover, the victim of an accident is entitled to claim 
compensation from either of the joint tortfeasors. Therefore, 
the finding of the Tribunal that the accident took place due to 
negligence of rider of the motorcycle to an extent of 30% is 
erroneous and needs interference.  
12. 
Learned counsel for the appellant vehemently 
contends that the Tribunal has not considered the income of 
the claimant properly. According to petition averments, he was 
earning Rs.17,500/- per month by working as an office boy in 
HDFC life insurance, as well as agriculturist. Even if he was 
unable to prove his income, the Tribunal ought to have taken 
the notional income of the claimant on the basis of chart 
prepared by the Karnataka State Legal Services Authority. It is

14. 
The Tribunal has taken the income of the claimant 
at Rs.8,000/- per month, assessed the disability at 20% to the 
whole body, applied multiplier of '16' and awarded a sum of 
Rs.3,07,200/- under the head loss of future earning capacity 
due to permanent disability. The notional income of the 
claimant, assessed by the Tribunal is on the lower side and 
hence requires enhancement. Since the accident occurred in 
the year 2017, the notional income as per the chart prepared 
by the Karnataka State Legal Services Authority can be taken 
as Rs.11,000/- per month. On the basis of the same, the 
compensation under the head loss of future earning capacity 
due to permanent disability requires recalculation. The 
compensation awarded under the heads 'pain and suffering' 
and 'food, nourishment and traveling charges' are on the lower 
side. Moreover, no compensation was awarded towards 'loss of
