2.
This petition questions the decision of the respondent –
Scrutiny Committee dated 15.04.2010, whereby the claim of the
petitioner of belonging to ‘Thakur, Scheduled Tribe’ has been turned
down. In support of his claim, the petitioner has relied upon following
pre-constitutional documents: (i) Attested xerox copy of primary school
leaving certificate of his father Shri. Laxman Mahadeo, issued by the
Headmaster, Municipal Council Primary Marathi School No. 1, Daryapur
Dist. Amravati, wherein the caste is mentioned as ‘Thakur’ on
19.06.1940. (ii) The extract of the primary school admission register of
petitioner’s real elder paternal uncle, Shri Shriram Mahadeo and father
Shri Laxman Mahadeo, issued by the Headmaster, Dr. Babasaheb
Ambedkar Municipal Council Higher Primary Marathi School No. 1
Daryapur, Dist. Amravati, wherein their caste is mentioned as ‘Thakur’
respectively on 03.07.1936 and 19.06.1940. Apart from these
documents, the petitioner relied upon 28 other documents in support of
his claim. Vigilance Cell has verified these documents during enquiry and
found them to be true. The pre-constitutional documents and other
documents are not accepted by the Committee on the ground that
candidate and his real elder paternal uncle have utterly failed in the
affinity test.
3.

upon the order passed by Nagpur Bench in the case of Digambar Shriram

“5.
The petitioner has produced in all 21 documents as

failure in affinity test, the caste claim has been rejected.
6.
The learned counsel appearing for petitioner relied on
the decision in case of Anand Vs. Committee for Scrutiny
and Verification of Tribe Claims (2012)(1) SCC 113, wherein
it is ruled that the affinity test may not be regarded as litmus
test for establishing the link of the petitioner with a
Scheduled Tribe. The affinity test may be used to
corroborate the documentary evidence and should not be
the sole criteria to reject a claim. Besides that the petitioner
relied on the decision of this Court Jaywant Dilip Pawar Vs.
State of Maharashtra & ors, 2018 (5) ALL MR 975 (S.C.),
wherein the area restriction has been removed.
7.
We have examined the impugned order which does
not assign substantial reasons to discard the pre-
constitutional documents of which genuineness has not been
doubted. Particularly document of the year 1936 bears a
primacy. It is well settled that more importance shall be
given to the pre-constitutional documents. Besides that
series of documents have been produced showing the entry
of “Thakur” caste. No adverse documents have been
procured and thus, it is difficult to accept the view expressed
by the Committee.

8.
Having regard to the pre-constitutional documents
and other material, we are of the considered opinion that
the Committee failed in error in rejecting the petitioner’s
caste claim. In view of that, petition deserves to be allowed,
hence following order :-
(I) Petition is allowed. We hereby quash and set aside
the impugned order 24.01.2023 passed by respondent
No.1 Committee.
(II) We hereby declare that the petitioner has duly
established that he belongs to "Thakur" Scheduled
Tribe which is enlisted at Serial No. 44 in the
Constitution (Scheduled Tribes) Order, 1950
(III) Respondent No.1 Committee is directed to issue
caste validity certificate to the petitioner of "Thakur"
Scheduled Tribe within four weeks from the date of
communication of this order.
(IV) Petition stands disposed of in above terms.”

Dattatraya was also dismissed. He, therefore, submits that in this view of

Replying the said argument, learned advocate for the

6.
It is pertinent to note that in Nagpur Bench decision in
Dattatraya’s case there is no reference of any pre-constitutional
document. 
7.
From the order passed in Digambar’s case it is evident that
pre-constitutional documents of petitioner’s father  Laxman dated
19.06.1940 and of his paternal uncle Shriram dated 03.07.1936 wherein
their caste is mentioned as ‘Thakur’ were considered by this Court and it
is also observed that only because the petitioner therein failed in affinity
test, his caste claim was rejected.
8.
Another ground on which the Committee has rejected the
claim of the petitioner is area restriction. The legal position is settled on
this point after the judgment in the case of Jaywant Dilip Pawar Vs. State

of Maharashtra & Ors.2 holding that the condition of area restriction is

9.
The Committee has also rejected the claim of the petitioner
on the ground that surnames of the petitioner’s community are Pawar,
Nikam, Chauhan, Shinde, Ingle, Wagh, Thakur, Kalse, etc. which are not
the surnames of ‘Thakur, Scheduled Tribe’. In view of pre-constitutional
documents and the decision in Digambar’s case, this ground is not
sufficient to reject the claim of the petitioner.
10.
For the above-mentioned reasons, the impugned decision of
the Committee cannot be sustained. Hence, the following order :
