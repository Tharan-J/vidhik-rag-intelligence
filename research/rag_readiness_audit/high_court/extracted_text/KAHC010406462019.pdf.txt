J U D G M E N T
Challenging judgment and award dated 16.01.2019 passed 
by the X Additional Judge, Court of Small Causes & MACT,9scch-
16),  Bengaluru, (hereinafter referred to as ‘Tribunal’ for short) 
in MVC No.2261/2016.  
MFA No.5171/2019 is filed by petitioner/appellant seeking 
enhancement of compensation; whereas MFA No.2729/2019 is 
filed by Insurer/appellant challenging liability fastened on it.  
2. 
For the sake of convenience, parties will be referred 
to as per their respective ranks before the tribunal. 
3. 
Brief facts as stated are that on 18.10.2015, 
petitioner Kusumakshi was walking by the side of Hulimavu Main 
Road, Bengaluru. At about 5:30 p.m. when she was in front of 
State Bank ATM, driver of Tata Winger vehicle bearing 
registration No. KA-02-AD-3826 drove it in rash and negligent 
manner and dashed against her. Due to the accident, she 
sustained grievous injuries. She was admitted to KIMS Hospital 
initially, thereafter took treatment at various other hospitals. 
Despite treatment, she sustained permanent physical deficiency. 
As on the date of accident she was 28 years of age, working as

11. Reg. point No.1: 
The accident occurred on 18.10.2015 at about 5:30 p.m. 
on busy Hulimavu road during peak traffic hours. It is stated that 
offending vehicle sped away without stopping after causing 
accident and petitioner was unable to note its registration 
number. Therefore, complaint was registered against an 
unknown vehicle. But, accident occurred in front of ATM of State 
Bank.  During course of their investigation, police collected CCTV 
footages from the ATM. 
12. On perusal of the same, it was seen that offending 
vehicle had photograph of Basavanna etc. Based on identification 
marks found in CCTV video, suspecting its involvement, insured 
vehicle was seized. On confrontation during investigation, driver 
of insured vehicle accepted to have caused the accident. Based 
on said admission, charge sheet was filed against driver of 
insured vehicle.  
13.  The Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Anita 
Sharma V. New India Assurance Co. Ltd., reported in 
(2021) 1 SCC 171, has held that in case of Motor accident 
claims, standard of proof is one of preponderance of probabilities 


--- PAGE 11 ---
11 
and strict rules of evidence do not apply. Therefore, petitioner 
need not establish negligence against driver of offending vehicle 
beyond reasonable doubt, as in criminal cases. The police which 
is an independent investigating authority has after due 
investigation, filed charge sheet against driver of insured vehicle. 
Though, counsel for insurer vehemently contended that CCTV 
footages were inconclusive insofar as vehicle registration number 
is concerned, but the investigating officer has stated that the 
insured vehicle matched with the identification marks with that 
of the offending vehicle in the CCTV images. Apart from the 
above evidence, admission by the driver of insured vehicle 
sufficiently probablised involvement of the insured vehicle in the 
accident.  
14.  In case, the insurer or the insured chose to dispute 
the same, it was incumbent upon them to establish beyond 
reasonable doubt that the insured vehicle was not involved in 
the accident, such as, by examining driver, owner or any 
independent witnesses, who could depose about presence of the 
insured vehicle at any place other than accident spot. The 
insurer is lacking in effort to verify status and result of criminal 


--- PAGE 12 ---
12 
prosecution against the driver of the insured vehicle. Therefore, 
adverse inference is required to be drawn.  
15.  The tribunal albeit on different reasons, answered 
issue no.1 in affirmative against the insurer. Same is neither 
capricious nor perverse, hence, said finding is confirmed.  
16. Reg. point no.2:  
Though there is a meek challenge to the assessment of 
compensation by the tribunal, the thrust of insurer’s challenge 
on quantum is, insofar as award of compensation towards ‘loss 
of income during laid up period’, as the assessment of ‘loss of 
earning capacity’ was total.   
17. On the other hand, petitioner is seeking enhancement 
of compensation on the ground of future prospects, as the 
assessment of functional disability was 100%. It is also 
contended that the gross monthly income i.e., Rs.7,166/- was 
required to be considered and not the net income after deduction 
of ESI contribution.  
18.  It is settled law that while assessing compensation 
towards loss of future earning or dependency, gross salary has 
to be considered after deduction of income tax and professional 


--- PAGE 13 ---
13 
tax. Therefore, Rs.7,166/- has to be considered as income of the 
petitioner.  
19.  Due to the injuries suffered in the accident, the 
petitioner has suffered 100% loss of earning capacity. In view of 
the law laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in  Kajal Vs. 
Jagadish Chand  reported in  (2020) 4 SCC 413, even in case 
of personal injury claims, future prospects are required to be 
added to the income of injured-petitioner. As the petitioner is 38 
years of age and doing private service, future prospects at 40% 
has to be added to her monthly income. The appropriate 
multiplier applicable is ‘15’. Thus, loss of future earning would 
be: 
Rs.7,166/- + 40% = Rs.10,032/- 
Rs.10,032/- X 12 X 15 X 100% =Rs.18,05,760/- 
Accordingly, 
petitioner 
is entitled 
to 
Rs.18,05,760/- 
towards ‘future earning capacity’.  
20.  As  rightly submitted by learned counsel for insurer, 
petitioner would not be entitled for ‘loss of income during laid up 
period’, in view of the disability being assessed at 100%. 
Therefore, award of Rs.42,258/- towards loss of income during 
laid up period is set-aside.

Reg. Point no.3 : 
The tribunal has awarded interest at the rate of 9% p.a.  
There are no reasons assigned for the same.  The rate of interest 
on term deposits in nationalised banks has reduced. Therefore, 
award of interest at 9% p.a. would be excessive. In usual 
course, rate of interest applicable to motor accident claims would 
be 6%. Thus, point no.3 is answered in the negative.
