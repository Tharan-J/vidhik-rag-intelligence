2. 
The award dated 18.03.2013 passed by the II Addl. 
Labour Court, Bengaluru in Reference No.02/2011 vide 
Annexure-W is called into question in this Writ Petition on 
several grounds as set out in the Memorandum of Writ Petition.  
3. 
Counsel for the respective parties urges several 
contentions. Heard the arguments and perused the Writ papers 
with care.  
 4. 
The short point that requires consideration is 
whether the award passed by the Labour Court requires 
interference.

5. 
Suffice it to note that the workman V.Krishnappa 
was appointed by the respondent on 08.10.1975, and he 
reached the scale of Driller/ Asst. Driller in the grade of JAE/ 
AAE, with whom his colleagues were confirmed. The respondent 
incurred heavy losses of over Rs 2 Crores, and the Government 
of Karnataka, in its order DDPER 55 ARU 2003 dated 
03.09.2003, ordered for the closure of activities and to relieve 
all the regular employees. Accordingly, the respondent closed 
its activities and relieved its 656 employees under the 
Voluntary Retirement Scheme 2003. It also closed all the 
branches in 2004. The regular employees opted for the VRS 
and submitted their voluntary retirement. The petitioner was 
one among them, and he was relieved on 31.10.2003 and 
received all the retirement benefits as worked out by the State 
Accounts Department. As the entitlements were settled, the 
relationship between the parties ceased. Strangely, the 
workman raised a dispute after a lapse of almost eight years in 
Ref. No.02/2011 before the Labour Court. The workman 
contended that the respondent Management forced him to opt 
for VRS, and there is a denial of monetary benefits. The Labour 
Court extenso referred to the material on record and, taking

note of the contentions urged on behalf of the respective 
parties, negatived the said contention. As far as the delay in 
raising the dispute, the Labour Court concluded that there is a 
deliberate delay on the part of the workman in approaching the 
Labour Court, and it is a stale dispute. In my view, the finding 
and the conclusion in rejecting the reference are just and 
proper. The reason is apparent. As already noted above, the 
workman was relieved in 2003, and the relationship had ceased 
to exist; hence, the contention that there was a force to opt for 
VRS must necessarily fail. Moreover, the Apex Court in 
PRABHAKAR 
V/S. 
JOINT 
DIRECTOR, 
SERICULTURE 
DEPARTMENT AND ANOTHER reported in (2015) 15 SCC 1 
has held that when there is no agitation by the workman for a 
long time and the dispute is raised belatedly and the delay and 
laches remained unexplained, it should be presumed that he 
had waived his right. It is also observed that at the time when 
the dispute was raised, it had become stale and was not an 
existing dispute. Even in the present case, when the dispute 
was raised by the workman, it had virtually become a stale 
dispute. There is an inordinate delay in raising the dispute. The 
Labour Court extenso referred to the material on record and
