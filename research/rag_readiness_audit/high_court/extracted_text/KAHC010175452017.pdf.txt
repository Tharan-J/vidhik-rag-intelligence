JUDGMENT 
Heard the learned counsel appearing for the appellants, 
the learned Additional Government Advocate for the first and 
second respondents and also the learned counsel appearing 
for the third respondent. 
 
2. 
These appeals take an exception to the order dated 2nd 
November 2016 passed by the learned Single Judge.  The 
present appeals are preferred by the third and fourth 
respondents to the writ petition filed by the third respondent in 
the appeals.   
 
3. 
An application for restoration under Section 5 of the 
Karnataka 
Scheduled 
Castes 
and 
Scheduled 
Tribes 
(Prohibition of Transfer of Certain Lands) Act, 1978 (for short 


--- PAGE 4 ---
 
 
 
 
- 4 - 
‘the said Act of 1978) was filed in the year 2006 by the 
appellants, who are the legal representatives of the original 
transferee.  The third respondent is a purchaser of the 
property.  The said application was disposed of by the 
Assistant Commissioner on 28th September 2012 by allowing 
the same.  An appeal was preferred by the third respondent.  
The appeal also came to be dismissed by the order dated 4th 
August 2014 and thus, the order of the Assistant Commissioner 
was confirmed. 
 
4. 
By the impugned order, the learned Single Judge came 
to the conclusion that the alienation has taken place on 2nd 
January 1971 in favour of the predecessor of the third 
respondent, while the grant was made on 16th February 1957 
and therefore, the alienation was not bad in law in view of the 
period of ten years of non-alienation mentioned in the order of 
grant.  The learned Single Judge, however, proceeded to set 
aside the order only as regards the land bearing survey 
No.46/3 measuring 1 acre and 6 guntas.   
 


--- PAGE 5 ---
 
 
 
 
- 5 - 
5. 
The learned counsel appearing for the appellants urged 
that the learned Single Judge has gone by the non-alienation 
period of ten years, but in fact, as per the relevant Rules, it was 
twenty five years.  His submission is that the Rule will prevail 
over the tenure mentioned in the grant.  Therefore, his 
contention is that the approach adopted by the learned Single 
Judge is erroneous. 
 
6. 
The learned counsel appearing for the third respondent 
pointed out that the said Act of 1978 came into force on 1st 
January 1979 and the application for restoration was made in 
the year 2006.  He relied upon the decision of the Apex Court 
in the case of NEKKANTI RAMA LAKSHMI vs. STATE OF 
KARNATAKA AND ANOTHER1, wherein it is held that though 
the said Act of 1978 does not prescribe any period of limitation, 
the application for restoration must be made within a 
reasonable time.  He urged that no interference is called for in 
view of the fact that there was unreasonable delay.

7. 
We have considered the submissions.  We must note 
here that the learned Single Judge has not specifically 
interfered with the order of the Assistant Commissioner and the 
Deputy Commissioner on the ground of delay. 
 
8. 
However, the law laid down by the Apex Court in various 
decisions, including the aforesaid decision in the case of 
Nekkanti Rama Lakshmi (supra) is well settled that an 
application for restoration under Section 5 of the said Act of 
1978 must be made within a reasonable time.  In the present 
case, the application was made twenty seven years after the 
said Act of 1978 came into force.  By no stretch of imagination, 
it can be said that the application was made by the appellants 
within a reasonable time. 
 
9. 
What was invoked before the learned Single Judge was 
his jurisdiction under Article 226 of the Constitution of India.  In 
our view, the order of restoration as regards survey No.46/3 
could not have been passed by the Assistant Commissioner 
and the Deputy Commissioner considering the long delay of 
twenty seven years in making the application from the date on

which the said Act came into force.  Therefore, this is not a fit 
case to interfere with the impugned order.  Accordingly, the 
appeals stand dismissed. 
 
  
The pending interlocutory application does not survive 
for consideration and stands disposed of.
