3.
An office memorandum is issued by the Government of India,
Ministry of Home Affairs on  20 May 2015 regarding revised
uniform guidelines for recruitment medical examination for
recruitment of GOs and NGOs in the CAPFs & AR.  Miscellaneous
Conditions of the revised uniform guidelines is in respect of tattoos
read thus :
“11. MISCELLANEOUS CONDITIONS -
……
……
3) Tattoo: The practice of engraving / tattooing in
India is prevalent since time immemorial, but has
been limited to depict the name or a religious
figure, invariably on inner aspect of forearm and
usually on left side.  On the other hand, the
present young generation is considerably under
the influence of western culture and thus the
number of potential recruits bearing skin art had
grown enormously over the years, which is not
only distasteful but distract from good order and
discipline in the force.

Following criteria are to be used to determine
permissibility of tattoo:
a) Content – being a secular country, the religious
sentiments of countrymen are to be respected and
thus tattoos depicting religious symbol or figure
and the name, as followed in Indian army, are to
be permitted. 
b) Location – tattoos marked on traditional sites
of the body like inner aspect of forearm, but only
LEFT forearm, being non saluting limb or
dorsum of hands are to be allowed. 
c) Size – size must be less than ¼ of the particular
part (Elbow or Hand) of body.
…… 
……”
Subsequently, on 16 July 2018, an amendment was carried out
in the uniform guidelines of medical examination which reads thus :
“In case a candidate has undergone removal of
tattoo (s) prior to appearing for recruitment process
and the same has faded substantially, this will be
treated as a “scar” and not a tattoo.  Such candidates
will be permitted to undergo the entire selection
process with the approval of Presiding Officer of the
recruitment Board.  Further, the scar resulting due
to removal of tattoo will be reviewed by the Medical
Board of Officer during Detailed Medical
Examination”.
4.
These office memorandums hold the field today are binding
not only on the Petitioner but also the Respondents since the
recruitment through public participation must strictly conform the
uniform rules.  Unnecessary dilution of the required criteria in a

particular case out of sympathy would cause injustice to those who

5.
Examination of the office memorandum would show that if
the candidate has undergone removal of tattoo prior to recruitment
process and it is faded substantially, then it will be considered as a
scar and not as a tattoo.  The scrutiny as per the office memorandum
is a factual one as to whether the tattoo is, (a) a religious tattoo;  (b) it
is on non-saluting arm; and (c) efforts must be made prior to
recruitment process by which the tattoo is faded substantially to be
treated as a scar.
6.
 Firstly, the Petitioner’s tattoo is on the left forearm, that on the
saluting arm.  Secondly, there is no specific statement that the tattoo
is religious one.  It is stated that the tattoo is as per tradition.  As to
what the tradition is not specifically stated.  An exception made from
the general rules of tattoo on the saluting arm is of religious symbol
or figure and the name. There has specific pleading in the petition
that the Petitioner’s tattoo is either religious symbol or figure and the
name, as followed in the Indian Army.  Merely by using the phrase
that it is a tradition is not sufficient.  Sufficient pleadings and  the
proof must be shown to fall within the exception.  Thirdly, there is
no statement that the tattoo was removed prior to recruitment
process.  Statement is made that tattoo is temporary, yet the
averment is also that it was taking almost a month for removal.   That

being the position, we find no reason to disbelieve that the

7.
The learned Counsel for the Petitioner has relied on the
decision of the Division Bench of this Court in the case of Shridhar
Mahadeo Pakhare Vs. Union of India and Others1.  In the said
decision, the Division Bench recorded a finding that the tattoo in
question was religious and it was removed upto  90%. Also this
decision is prior to the amendment to the Rules of 16 July 2018. The
learned Single Judge of High Court of Gauhati relied by the
Petitioner in the case of Mridul Baishya Vs. The Union of India and
Ors.2 followed the decision in the case of Shridhar Mahadeo Pakhare
and the learned Single Judge found that in that case also the tattoo
was removed upto 90% and it was a religious tattoo.  Further, the
amendment of July 2018 was not brought to the notice of the
learned Single Judge.  In these circumstances,  both these decisions
do not assist the Petitioner.
8.
Even otherwise, we are informed that the post for which the
Petitioner had applied has already been filled up and no effective
relief can be granted.
