2. 
The summary of the case of the complainant 
in the Trial Court is that the complainant company was 
manufacturing electrical cables.  The accused who was 
the 
customer 
of 
the 
complainant 
company, 
on 
01.07.2008 placed purchase order for supply of goods 
worth `13,79,947/-. Accordingly, on 24.07.2008, the 
complainant company delivered the goods to the 
accused under invoice No.0460 valued at `13,79,947/-.   
In order to pay the said price of the goods, the accused 
issued a cheque bearing No.260391 dated 14.07.2008 
for a sum of `1,00,000/-, another cheque bearing 
No.260390 dated 27.08.2008 for `2,00,000/- and 
cheque bearing No.   260392 dated 27.08.2008  for a 
sum of `10,79,947/-.  All the cheques were drawn on 
Axis Bank Ltd., Jayanagar Branch, 4th Block, Bengaluru

in favour of the complainant.  The first two cheques 
were honoured when presented for realisation by the 
complainant.  However, the third cheque for a sum of 
`10,79,947/- returned unpaid when presented for 
realisation for the reason of insufficiency of funds in the 
account of the drawer. Thereafter complainant got 
issued a legal notice upon the accused on 16.02.2009 
demanding the payment of the cheque amount.  
However, the accused instead of paying the cheque 
amount, sent an untenable reply dated 25.02.2009.  
This constrained the complainant to institute a criminal

under Section 138 of the N.I. Act in the Trial Court. 
 
Since the accused pleaded not guilty, the trial was

The accused No.2 got himself examined as DW-1. The 
Trial Court, after hearing arguments  and considering

7. 
The point that arises for my consideration 
is, “whether the Judgment of conviction and Order on 
sentence passed by the Trial Court and confirmed by 
the Sessions Judge’s Court is incorrect and suffers with 
any illegality or perversity, warranting interference at 
the hands of this Court?” 
 
 
8. 
According 
to 
the 
complainant, 
the 
complainant No.1 is a company and complainant No.2 is 
the Managing Director of the complainant No.1. The 
accused No.1 is a proprietory concern of which accused 
No.2 is the proprietor. 
 
 
It is not in dispute that the complainant and 
accused No.1 were known to each other and there was

business transaction between them.  It is also not in 
dispute that the cheque at Ex.P1 was drawn by the

cheque when presented for its realisation by the 
complainant came to be returned for the reason of 
insufficiency of funds as could be seen from the 
banker’s endorsement at Exs.P2 and P3.  It is also not 
in dispute that after the returning of the cheque, the 
complainant got issued a legal notice upon the accused 
as per Ex.P4 and after receipt of the said legal notice, 
the accused sent their reply as per Ex.P10. These 
undisputed facts which have come out in the evidence

counsels from both side in their arguments, forms a 
presumption in favour of the complainant about the 
existence of legally enforceable debt under Section 139 
of the N.I. Act.  However, the said presumption is 
rebuttable.

interest and there is a mentioning to that effect in the

Learned counsel for the complainant in his arguments 
fairly conceded that the said invoice at Ex.P12 nowhere 
mentions about the liability of the customers to pay any

Added to the above, the accused also in his 
examination-in-chief as D.W.1 has stated that he did 
not agree to pay any interest.  He has further stated

there was no agreement regarding the payment of the 
interest.  In such circumstances where there is no proof

the complainant or any agreement between them

`4,00,000/- received by the complainant towards the 
interest was uncalled for.   
 
Therefore, the entire amount of `4,00,000/- 
received by the complainant was required to be 
appropriated towards the total amount due of a sum of 
`13,79,947/- which is the invoice amount. After 
deducting `1,00,000/- in admitted received amount of 
Rupees Four lakhs in the total value of the invoice, the 
balance that remains as an alleged liability of the

of 
`9,79,947/- 
but 
not 
the 
cheque 
amount 
of 
`10,79,947/-.  This strengthens the defence taken up by 
the accused that the cheque at Ex.P1 was issued only as 
a security which was returnable after payment of the 
entire amount.  However, the complainant has misused 
the same by presenting it for its realisation. Thus when 
the alleged outstanding liability is established to be an 
amount which is lesser than the amount mentioned in 
the cheque at Ex.P1 as on the date of presentation of                  
the cheque, the liability could not have been the cheque 
amount of `10,79,947/-.

14. 
In a similar circumstances, this Court in 
Sami Labs Limited (supra),  was pleased to hold that 
Section 138 of the N.I. Act makes it mandatory that, to 
attract Section 138 of the N.I. Act making the dishonour 
of the cheque as an offence, the issuance of cheque 
must be as a whole or in part of any debt or other 
liability.  It was further observed in that Judgment that 
when any sum mentioned in the cheque is for a higher 
amount than the outstanding liability and if that excess 
amount is also not towards any interest, cost, penalty 
etc. but remains unexplained, then  Section 138 of the 
N.I. Act cannot be said to be applicable. 
 
 
15. 
In the instant case also as observed above, 
thought the cheque at Ex.P1 is for a sum of 
`10,79,947/- but the alleged outstanding liability of the 
accused towards the complainant as on the date of 
presentation of the cheque was only a sum of 
`9,79,947/- which is the amount less than the cheque 
amount, though the complainant contended that a sum 
of `1,00,000/- was appropriated towards the interest 


--- PAGE 17 ---
                                                                                             Crl.R.P.No.158/2018 
17 
 
 
but the analysis made above goes to show that no 
agreement with respect to payment of interest was 
entered into between the parties.  As such, unilaterally 
the complainant could not have appropriated the said 
amount of `1,00,000/- towards the alleged interest 
payable by the accused. Therefore, when the alleged 
outstanding liability as on the date of presentation of 
the cheque was lesser than the cheque amount it 
cannot be held that the cheque at Ex.P1 was issued by 
the 
accused 
towards 
the 
payment 
of 
a 
legally 
enforceable 
debt 
since 
there 
existed 
no 
legally 
enforceable debt to an amount equivalent to the cheque 
amount. As such, Section 138 of the N.I. Act cannot be 
pressed into service in such circumstances.  However, 
this aspect was not considered by the Trial Court as well 
as the Sessions Judge’s Court.  On the contrary both of 
them considering the fact that the cheque at Ex.P1 was 
drawn by the accused in favour of the complainant and 
the same came to be returned unpaid when presented 
for realisation and also considering the fact that the 
complainant had issued a demand notice demanding the 


--- PAGE 18 ---
                                                                                             Crl.R.P.No.158/2018 
18 
 
 
payment of the cheque amount, have proceeded to hold 
the accused guilty of the alleged offence.  Since the said 
finding of the Trial Court as well the Sessions Judge’s 
Court now proved to be perverse, interference in the 
matter is warranted.   
Accordingly, I proceed to pass the following:
