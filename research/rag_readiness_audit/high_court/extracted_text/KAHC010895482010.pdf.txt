JUDGMENT 
 
1.  
The claimants are in appeal challenging the exoneration 
of the liability of the insurer and they are seeking for 
enhancement of compensation awarded by the Tribunal.  
 
2. 
Two claim petitions were filed contending that deceased 
Puttasidda @ Jutti, was a coolie by profession (in MVC 
No.1169/2006) and deceased Mahadevaiah @ Rajanna, a 
cleaner in auto rickshaw (in MVC No.1170/2006), were 
travelling in a auto rickshaw bearing registration No.KA.50/413 
on 28.07.2006.  It was stated that said Puttasidda was engaged 
for the work of pasting advertising posters for opening of 
Varma T.C.H. College, Kollegal, along with other coolies, Dore, 
Nagendra and the cleaner of goods auto - Mahadevaiah.  It is 
stated that Varma T.C.H. College was a newly constituted 
college and for that purpose they were working in relation to 
the pasting of the advertisement posters.  It was contended 
that while they were going in a auto rickshaw from Kollegal to 
B.R.Hills, the goods auto rickshaw got overturned and as a

result, Puttasidda and Mahadevaiah, who had been engaged for 
pasting the advertisement posters were killed.  It was 
contended that they were entitled for compensation.  
 
3. 
The claimants have arrayed the owner of the auto 
rickshaw and the driver of auto rickshaw as respondent Nos.1 
and 2 in both the petitions and they also arrayed insurer as 
respondent No.3. 
 
4. 
Tribunal after assessing the evidence concluded that the 
claimants were entitled to a sum of Rs.1,54,600/- (in MVC 
No.1169) and Rs.3,03.800/- (in MVC No.1170).  Tribunal, but 
however, came to the conclusion that the insurer was not liable 
to pay the compensation since there was no evidence to show 
that Mahadevaiah was the cleaner.  Tribunal also held that 
deceased Puttasidda had not been proved to be employed by 
the owner or the hirer and therefore, insurer would not be 
liable to pay the compensation.  
 
5. 
Learned counsel for the claimants contended that no 
doubt the seating capacity of the auto rickshaw was stated as 
'1' in the registration certificate, but, however contended that

the registered laden weight of the auto rickshaw being 990 
kilos, as per the proviso to Rule 100 of the Motor Vehicle Rules, 
1989, the goods auto rickshaw could carry a maximum of 3 
persons.  He contended that what was prescribed in the 
registration certificate was only the seating capacity and not 
the carrying capacity.  It was his assertion that in view of the 
permissibility of persons being allowed to be carried in a goods 
vehicle under the proviso to Rule 100 of Motor Vehicle Rules, 
the Tribunal had erred in coming to the conclusion that insurer 
was not liable to pay the compensation.   
 
6. 
Learned counsel for the owner contended that, as a 
matter of fact, in order to avoid litigation, a sum of 
Rs.1,50,000/- had been paid to the claimants and same was 
accepted by them and therefore they could not lay claim for 
further compensation.   
 
7. 
Sri.B.Pradeep, learned counsel for the insurer contended 
that since the seating capacity prescribed was only '1', it was 
wholly impermissible for any passengers to be carried in a 
goods auto rickshaw and as a consequence, there would be no

liability on the insurer at all.  He places strong reliance on the

GADHILINGAPPA @ GADHILINGA vs. GULEPPA - AIR 
2021 ACJ 2588. 
 
8. 
Learned counsel for the claimants also contended that the 
document produced by the owner of the offending vehicle

be acted upon and same was also not admitted by the

by the claimants the owner of the offending vehicle cannot be 
permitted to contend that compensation had been paid.  He 
also submitted that even if the documents produced is 
accepted, it would indicate that monies paid or agreed to be 
paid was for the purpose of withdrawing of the criminal case 
and had no relation to claim for compensation.  
 
9. 
As far as the liability questioned is concerned, it is to be 
noticed here that as per the 'B' Register Extract produced

before the Tribunal at Ex.R-3, the seating capacity of the auto 
rickshaw has been stated as '1' and the registered laden weight 
is stated as 990 kilos.  Rule 100 of the Karnataka Motor 
Vehicles Rules, 1989, states that as a general rule no person 
shall be carried in a goods vehicle.  However, a proviso is 
appended to the sub-rule, which states that the owner  or  the  
hirer  or  a  bonafide  employee  of  the  owner  or  the  hirer  
of  the vehicle can be carried free of charge so long as the total 
number of persons so carried does not exceed 3 in case of a 
light transport goods vehicle.   
 
10. 
It is not in dispute that vehicle in question is a light 
transport goods vehicle having registered laden weight of 990 
kilos and therefore, under the proviso to Rule 100 of the Motor 
Vehicle Rules, it was permissible for 3 passengers to be carried 
in a goods vehicle.  In other words, by virtue of the proviso, 
notwithstanding of the seating capacity of just '1', since the 
Rule permits carriage of persons in a goods vehicle, the 
carriage of certain persons in a goods vehicle would not be per 
se illegal.   
 


--- PAGE 10 ---
 
- 10 -      
 
MFA No. 10721 of 2010 
   
 
 
   
C/W MFA No. 10723 of 2010    
 
11. 
The persons who can be carried in a goods vehicle are 
clearly prescribed as either the owner or the hirer.  Similarly, 
even the bonafide employee of the owner or hirer of the vehicle 
are also be permitted to be carried in the goods vehicle free of 
charge.  Thus, statutorily, in a goods vehicle the carriage of 
persons is permitted under certain specified conditions.  
 
12. 
To put it differently, even if the registration certificate 
contains a prescription about the seating capacity of a vehicle 
that would not over-ride the statutory provision, which enables 
carriage of passengers.  Thus, in my view, it cannot be denied 
that certain persons can be carried in the goods vehicle and if 
the law permits carriage of persons, the insurer would be 
automatically liable so long as the persons being carried in the 
vehicle specified with the conditions with the statutory 
provisions.   
 
13. 
It is further contended that sub-rule (4) of Rule 100 of 
Motor Vehicle Rules prohibits the carriage of any persons unless  
an  area  of  not  less  than  0.40  sq.mts.  of  the  floor  of  the  
vehicle  is  kept  open  for each person.  In the instant case, it

is not in dispute that the vehicle in question is a good vehicle 
and the space available for carriage of the goods at the rear would 
be definitely more than 0.40 sq.mts and it is easily possible for 
passengers to travel in the area earmarked for carriage of goods.   
 
14. It is to be stated here that the Rule 100 does not 
prohibit the carriage of passengers in the area earmarked 
for carriage of goods. In other words, in the area ear 
marked for carriage of goods, if there is adequate space, 
passengers can also be carried provided they satisfy the 
conditions prescribed in Rule 100 of Karnataka Motor 
Vehicles Rules, 1989 (for short hereinafter referred to as 
'Rule 100'). 
 
15. The argument of the learned counsel for the insurer 
that since the registration certificate prescribes a specified 
seating capacity, no passengers can be carried, cannot be 
accepted.

16. Reliance is placed on Form 23 of the Central Motor 
Vehicle Rules, in which, the seating capacity is prescribed. 
It is therefore, contended that only those persons 
permitted to be seated as per the R.C. can alone be 
carried. 
 
17. As stated above, the statutory Rule has been framed 
keeping in mind that as a normal Rule, carriage of 
passengers in a goods vehicle is impermissible but 
exceptions can be provided for.  The legislature was aware 
that though the vehicle was not designed to provide for a 
seating capacity other than the driver, but yet, the Rule 
was framed in such a way that it permitted the carriage of 
passengers even in a  goods vehicle, provided they satisfy 
the conditions prescribed therein.  This argument therefore 
would be of no avail. 
 
18. The reliance placed on the Full Bench decision of this 
Court [Gadhilingappa @ Gadhilinga Vs. K.Guleppa - 
AIR 
(Karnataka)

by 
Sri.B.Pradeep,

counsel, would also be of no consequence, since in the 
said Full Bench decision related to the specific question as 
to whether a person travelling on the mud guard of a 
tractor can be construed as an authorized passenger or an 
unauthorized passenger or whether the liability of such 
person is covered or not?. 
 
19. Indisputably, Rule 100 was not considered by the Full 
Bench and further more, in that particular case, the Full 
Bench took the view that by reason of Regulation 28 which 
prohibited the tractor driver from carrying or along with 
any person on the tractor, it would thereby disentitle any 
person to claim compensation, if he was found to be 
travelling on the mud guard.   
 
20. However, in the instant case, in respect of the light 
goods vehicle, since the Rule itself provides for carriage of 
passengers, the decision of the Full Bench can have no 
application.

21. The reliance placed upon the judgment rendered by 
the Supreme Court in United India Insurance Co. Ltd., 
Vs. Suresh K K - LAWS(SC)-2008-4-10, which is to the 
effect that the policy contemplates the liability of the 
owner alone and not any other person cannot be pressed 
into service, since in that particular case, the Supreme 
Court was not considering a Rule which is similar to Rule 
100.  
 
22. As stated above, in the State of Karnataka, Rule 100 
permits carriage of passengers in a goods vehicle under 
specified conditions. Since the Supreme Court was not 
considering such a situation, the said decision can be of no 
assistance. 
 
23. Consequently, since the carriage of passengers in the 
instant case was within the permissible limit of Rule 100, 
the liability of the insurance company to satisfy the 
compensation would be attracted.  However, since there is 
a breach of the policy conditions, the insurer can
