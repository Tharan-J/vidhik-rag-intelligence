ORDER 
 
This 
petition 
has 
been 
filed 
by 
the 
petitioner/accused No.2 under Section 438 of the 
Criminal Procedure Code to release him on anticipatory 
bail in the event of his arrest in connection with Crime 
No.10/2019 of Khanapur Police Station registered for 
the offence punishable under Section 379 of the Indian 
Penal Code, 1860. 
2. 
I have heard the learned counsel for the 
petitioner and the learned High Court Government 
Pleader appearing for the respondent-State. 
3. 
The gist of the complaint is that on 
19.12.2018, at about 9.30 a.m., the complainant and 
his son went to the New Bus Stand on their motor cycle 
bearing registration No.KA-22 S-5177 and parked the 
same by the side of Ambedkar Garden Compound at 


--- PAGE 3 ---
  
 
 
: 3 : 
New Khanapur Bus Stand. Thereafter, they went to 
their work at Haliyal and Londa and after they returned 
to Khanapur at about 6.00 p.m., they did not find their 
motor cycle and later came to know that the same was 
stolen. On the basis of the said complaint a case has 
been registered. 
4. It is the submission of the learned counsel for 
the petitioner that though the alleged incident has 
taken place on 19.12.2018, the complaint was lodged on 
16.01.2019 and thereby there is an inordinate delay in 
filing the complaint. It is also his submission that the 
petitioner/accused has not committed any crime and he 
is not a ‘habitual offender’ as defined under the 
Karnataka Habitual Offenders Act, 1961 (for short, ‘the 
Act’). 
It 
is 
his 
further 
submission 
that 
the 
petitioner/accused has been falsely implicated in 
several cases one after the other; the alleged offence is 
not punishable either with death or imprisonment for

: 4 : 
life; already investigation is completed and charge-sheet 
has been filed; the accused is ready to abide by any 
conditions that may be imposed by this Court and is 
also ready to offer sureties. On these grounds, he 
prayed to allow the petition and to release the petitioner 
on anticipatory bail. 
5. 
Per 
contra, 
the 
learned 
High 
Court 
Government Pleader vehemently argued and submitted 
that there is prima facie material as against the 
petitioner/accused for having committed the alleged 
offence and the petitioner/accused is involved in many 
more criminal cases of similar nature relating to theft of 
motor cycle; he is a habitual offender and if he is 
released by granting anticipatory bail, he may tamper 
with the prosecution witness, he may abscond and may 
not be available for trial, and he may commit similar 
offences. On these grounds, he prayed to dismiss the 
petition.

: 5 : 
6.  
I have carefully and cautiously considered 
the submissions made by the learned counsel for the 
parties and perused the records. 
7. 
The alleged offence is not punishable either 
with death or imprisonment for life. The Trial Court has 
come to the conclusion that the petitioner is a ‘habitual 
offender’. A ‘habitual offender’ has been defined in the 
Act under Section 2(e) which reads as under: 
“2. Definitions.—In this Act, unless the 
context otherwise requires,—  
(a) x x x x x x x x x x 
(b) x x x x x x x x x x  
(e) “habitual offender” means a person 
who, during any continuous period of five years, 
whether before or after the commencement of 
this Act, or partly before and partly after such 
commencement, 
has 
been 
sentenced 
on 
conviction on not less than three occasions, 
since he attained the age of eighteen years, to a 
substantive term of imprisonment, for any one 
or more of the scheduled offences, committed on 


--- PAGE 6 ---
  
 
 
: 6 : 
different 
occasions 
and 
not 
so 
connected 
together as to form part of the same transaction, 
such sentence not having been reversed in 
appeal or on revision: 
 Provided 
that 
in 
computing 
the 
continuous period of five years referred to above, 
any period spent in jail either under sentence of 
imprisonment or under detention shall not be 
taken into account;” 
A close reading of the said section makes it clear that in 
order to call a person a ‘habitual offender’, he should be 
sentenced on conviction for not less than three occasions 
after he attains the age of majority. But, in the case on 
hand, 
no 
such 
orders 
convicting 
the 
present 
petitioner/accused have been passed so as to call him a 
‘habitual offender’. Even though the learned High Court 
Government Pleader contended that many more cases 
have 
been 
registered 
against 
the 
present 
petitioner/accused and he is a ‘habitual offender’, with 
reference to Section 2(e) of the Act referred to above, he 
cannot be called as a ‘habitual offender’. Under the facts 


--- PAGE 7 ---
  
 
 
: 7 : 
and circumstances of the case, if the petitioner is 
released on anticipatory bail by imposing stringent 
conditions, the same would meet the ends of justice. 
 
8. 
In the light of the above discussion, the 
petition is allowed. The petitioner/accused No.2 is 
ordered to be released on anticipatory bail, in the event of 
his arrest, in connection Crime No.10/2019 of Khanapur 
Police Station, subject to following conditions: 
(a) the petitioner shall execute a personal bond for a 
sum of Rs.1,00,000/- with two sureties for the 
likesum to the satisfaction of the Investigating 
Officer; 
(b) he shall surrender before the Investigating Officer 
within fifteen days from today; 
(c) he shall mark his attendance once in fifteen days 
till the trial is concluded 
(d) he shall not leave the jurisdiction of the Court 
without prior permission; and

(e) he shall not indulge in similar type of criminal 
activities. If he is found to have indulged in 
similar type of criminal activities, the respondent-
