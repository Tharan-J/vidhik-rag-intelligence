It is the case of the plaintiff that the plaintiff is the 
daughter of Doddobappa (second son of Hanumappa) and she 
was adopted by Huchobappa who died issueless.  It is further 
stated that there was a partition amongst the children of Late

Survey No.46/5 measuring 1 acre 25 guntas was allotted in

Obappa alias Obliga (fourth son of Hanumappa) sold an extent of 
4 acre 23 guntas Sy.No.46/3 in favour of one Papaiah as per 
registered sale deed dated 08th October, 1946.  It is the case of 
the plaintiff that the schedule in the two registered sale deeds 
dated 08th October, 1946 was wrongly shown and since the 
plaintiff is the adoptive daughter of Huchobappa, the purchaser 
Papaiah has filed O.S.No.43 of 1968 alleging that property in

further stated that the schedule and survey number has been

gunta.  It is the case of the plaintiff that, the plaintiff has

1. Whether both the Courts below are right in law in

13.  In the light of the above, perusal of the record would 
clearly indicate that there is no dispute with regard to the 
relationship between the parties.  Originally, the schedule land 
belonged to propositus-Hanumappa and thereafter, devolved in 
favour of his five sons.  Fifth son died as bachelor and therefore, 
no share was assigned in his favour.  It is pertinent to mention 
that Huchobappa died leaving behind his wife Basamma and she 
also died issues.  The case of the plaintiff is that she has been 
given in adoption by her natural parents Doddobappa to 
Huchobappa long back and their custom provides for adoption.  
In this connection, careful examination of the record would 
substantiate the fact that no adoption took place as averred by 
the plaintiff.  On the other hand, though the plaintiff has 
examined PW2 and PW3, however, the testimonies of these 
witnesses cannot be considered to be a basis for arriving at a 
conclusion 
that 
the 
plaintiff 
was 
given 
in 
adoption 
to 


--- PAGE 12 ---
 
12
Huchobappa.  The plaintiff failed to prove before the trial Court 
with regard to customary practice relating to adoption.  It is also 
pertinent to mention that the sale deed and partition deed, 
which is the basis for deciding the lis between the parties was 
not produced by the plaintiff and on the other hand, sale deed 
and partition deed have been produced by the defendants to 
prove their legitimate right in respect of the suit schedule 
property.  The RTC extract, though presumptive in nature, would 
clearly establish the fact that the names of Shivagangamma and 
Shanamma were shown in the revenue records and same has 
been incorporated in terms of the gift deed and as such, though 
the testimony of DW1 to DW3 have been cross-examined at 
length, however, the plaintiff failed to elicit the contra evidence 
and all the evidence of DW1 to DW3 was consistent and constant 
and same cannot be disbelieved and in that view of the matter, 
the finding recorded by the trial Court at paragraphs 14 and 15 
of the judgment is to be affirmed.  The trial Court has 
appreciated the documents in threadbare and arrived at a 
conclusion that the plaintiff who is the defendant in O.S No.43 of 
1968, has failed to take a stand in her written statement that 


--- PAGE 13 ---
 
13
she has been given in adoption to Huchhobappa.  She also 
admits her residential address as a daughter of Doddaobappa 
and if at all she has been adopted by Huchhobappa, she could 
have taken a plea in the written statement with regard to 
adoption and in that view of the matter, the finding recorded the 
trial Court is just and proper and cannot be interfered in this 
appeal.  I have also noticed that the item No.3-Sy.no.46/5 
measuring 1 acre 25 guntas, is not given to the plaintiff at any 
point of time and therefore, the finding recorded by the trial 
court is just and proper. 
 
  
14.  I have also examined the finding recorded by the 
First Appellate Court wherein the First Appellate Court having re-
appreciated the material on record, particularly the evidence of 
PW1 and Exhibit P34 with regard to Sy.no.46/2, has held that 
the said document does not fortify the contention of the 
appellant that she was taken in adoption by Huchhobappa and 
his wife Basamma.  The First Appellate Court has also taken note 
of the fact that defendants acquired title to Sy.no.46/3 as per 
sale deed dated 08th October, 1946 and after reappreciating the 


--- PAGE 14 ---
 
14
evidence on record had come to a conclusion that the plaintiff 
failed to prove the same since the burden is on the plaintiff to 
establish that the vendor of the defendant has purchased Survey 
No.46/3 and Sy.no.46/5 and in this regard, the defendants have 
produced the sale deed at Exhibit D1 and after re-appreciating 
the material on record and taking into consideration the 
compromise entered into between the  parties as per Ex.P19 in 
O.S No.43 of 1968 has rightly come to a conclusion that the 
plaintiff failed to place clear, cogent and consistent evidence to 
substantiate that Survey number was wrongly mentioned in 
Exhibits D1 to D19 and therefore, I do not find any material to 
interfere with the impugned judgment and decree passed by the 
Courts below and as such, the substantial question of law framed 
above is answered in favour of the defendants.  It is also settled 
principle of law that power of this Court in second appeal under 
Section 100 of Code of Civil Procedure is limited solely to decide 
a substantial question of law, if at all, same arises in the case.  
In this case, since both the Courts have concurrently recorded a 
finding against the plaintiff and therefore, taking into account 
the finding recorded by both the Courts below, I do not find any

15
illegality or  perversity to interfere with the impugned judgment 
and decree passed by the Courts below.  Hence, appeal is 
dismissed as the same is devoid of merits.
