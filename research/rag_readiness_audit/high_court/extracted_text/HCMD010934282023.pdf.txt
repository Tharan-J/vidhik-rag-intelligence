O R D E R
This petition is filed seeking quashment of FIR registered against 
the petitioner for the offences under Sections 143, 341, 283 of IPC and 
Sections 41(A) and 71A(1) of the Tamil Nadu City Police Act, 1888.

2.According to the prosecution, on 15.11.2019 at about 04.00 p.m. 
when the respondent police were patrolling near Gandhi Market 
Vellamandi and Ramakrishna Bridge, the petitioner along with 133 
members assembled without obtaining prior permission causing 
disturbance to the public. Thereby respondent police have registered FIR 
in Crime No.664 of 2019 against the petitioner and others for the 
offences punishable under Sections 143, 341, 283 of IPC and Sections 
41(A) and 71A(1) of the Tamil Nadu City Police Act, 1888.
3. It is submitted by learned counsel for the petitioner that the 
petitioner and others were protested peacefully without causing 
disturbance to anybody and as a part of his business the petitioner needs 
to travel abroad thereby, sought for quashment of FIR. It is also 
submitted that three years have been lapsed from the date of alleged 
occurrence of offences thereby taking cognizance is hit by Section 468 of 
Cr.P.C.
4. Heard both sides and perused the record.

5. The petitioner/accused was charged for five offences. The 
punishment for the offence under Section 143 of IPC is imprisonment for 
a term which may extend to six months; the punishment for the offence 
under Section 341 of IPC is imprisonment for a term which may extend 
to one month; the punishment for the offence under Section 283 of IPC is 
fine which may extend to two hundred rupees; the punishment for the 
offence under Section 41(A) of the Tamil Nadu City Police Act is 
imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year; finally, the 
punishment for the offence under  Section 71A(1) of the Tamil Nadu 
City Police Act is imprisonment for a term which may extend to three 
months. Considering the above periods of sentences that can be awarded 
in case the offences are proved against the petitioner/accused, the 
maximum sentence that can be awarded is one year for the offence under 
Section 41(A) of the Tamil Nadu City Police Act. 
6. Section 468 of Cr.P.C. runs as under:
“468. Bar to taking cognizance after lapse of the period of 
limitation.

7. Considering the above provisions, the charge sheet under 
Sections 143, 341, 283 of IPC and Sections 41(A) and 71A(1) of the 
Tamil Nadu City Police Act, 1888., should have been filed within one 
year. The offences are committed on 15.11.2019, therefore, the charge 
sheet should have been filed on or before 14.11.2020. However, in the 
present case, the respondent police has not filed charge sheet as of today 
which is beyond one year thereby taking cognizance for the offences is 
barred under Section 468 of Cr.P.C.

8. The Hon'ble Supreme Court has laid down the guidelines for 
exercising of inherent power under Section 482 of Cr.P.C. for quashing 
the criminal proceeding in the case of State of Haryana v. Bhajanlal 
reported in 1992 SCC (Cri) 426, which reads as follows:-
"102. In the backdrop of the interpretation of the various 
relevant provisions of the Code under Chapter XIV and of the 
principles of law enunciated by this Court in a series of 
decisions relating to the exercise of the extraordinary power 
under Article 226 or the inherent powers under Section 482 of 
the Code which we have extracted and reproduced above, we 
give the following categories of cases by way of illustration 
wherein such power could be exercised either to prevent abuse 
of the process of any court or otherwise to secure the ends of 
justice, though it may not be possible to lay down any precise, 
clearly defined and sufficiently channelised and inflexible 
guidelines or rigid formulae and to give an exhaustive list of 
myriad kinds of cases wherein such power should be exercised. 
(1) Where the allegations made in the first 
information report or the complaint, even if they are taken at 
their face value and accepted in their entirety do not prima 
facie constitute any offence or make out a case against the 
accused. 
(2) Where the allegations in the first information 
report and other materials, if any, accompanying the FIR do

9. In view of the above principles laid down by the Hon'ble Apex 
Court, if the continuation of proceedings are barred by any statute or law, 
such proceedings required to be quashed. In the case on hand, taking 
cognizance under all the offences alleged against the petitioner are 
specifically barred under Section 468(2)(b) of Cr.P.C. Thereby the FIR 
registered against the petitioner shall not sustain.
10. In the result, this criminal original petition is allowed and the 
proceedings in Crime No.664 of 2019 on the file of the respondent 
police, are hereby quashed. Consequently, connected miscellaneous 
petition is closed.
