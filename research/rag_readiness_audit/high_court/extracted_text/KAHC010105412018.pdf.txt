This writ petition is filed under Articles-226 & 227 of the 
Constitution of India, praying to quash the impugned order dated 
08.06.2018 issued by the 5th respondent vide Annexure-H.  
Consequently, quash the impugned notice dated 20.07.2018 
issued by the 5th respondent at Annexure-J and to direct the 5th  
respondent to permit the petitioner to continue the quarry 
operation in the schedule property.   
 
These writ petitions, having being heard and reserved, 
coming on for pronouncement of order, this day, the Chief 
Justice pronounced the following: 
  
O R D E R 
 
          As the facts of these petitions are more or less similar, for 
the convenience, we are referring to facts of the case in 
W.P.31590/2018. 
 
2. 
The petitioner was granted a quarrying lease on 3rd July 
2009 in respect of building stone.  The quarrying lease was 
granted pursuant to the notification dated 28th May 2009 issued 
under Rule 27 of the Karnataka Minor Mineral Concession Rules, 
1994 (for short ‘the said Rules of 1994’).  The lease was for a 


--- PAGE 12 ---
12 
 
period of ten years.  The main challenge in this petition is to the 
order dated 22nd August 2019 by which for violation of Rule 19-A 
of the said Rules of 1994 has been alleged and the lease has 
been ordered to be kept under suspension.   
 
3. 
This petition has been initially filed for challenging the order 
dated 08th June 2018 (Annexure-N) passed by the 5th respondent 
(Senior Geologist).  By the said order, it was observed that the 
land subject matter of the lease was within radius of ten 
kilometers of the Bannerghatta National Park.  It was alleged that 
the mining was not done in terms of the said quarrying lease in a 
scientific manner by creating benches as per the approved 
quarrying plan. It was observed that the petitioner has not 
followed the terms and conditions specified in environmental 
clearance. Further, it was observed that the petitioner has not 
produced a licence for use of explosives.  Therefore, a direction 
was issued to keep the mining activities in abeyance till a 
decision was taken in the meeting of Task Force Committee  
under the Chairmanship of the Deputy Commissioner. The 
petition was further amended in terms of order passed on I.A-
1/2018 for incorporating a challenge to the notice dated 18th/20th 


--- PAGE 13 ---
13 
 
July 2018 issued by the 5th respondent.  The said notice was 
issued based on the joint inspection conducted on 5th June 2018.  
Three omissions were noted in the said notice which are as 
under: 
“A.  
The boundary stones of the leased area are 
not put up as provided in the said Rules of 
1994; 
 B. 
Mining work has not been carried out 
scientifically by creating benches as per the 
quarrying plan and  
C.  
There is a violation of conditions in the 
environmental clearance.”  
 
 
As per the said notice dated 18th/20th July 2018 (Annexure-
P), the petitioner was called upon to submit explanation within 
thirty days, failing which quarrying lease may be cancelled as per 
Rule 45 of the said Rules of 1994.  By the said notice, the 
petitioner was called upon to stop the mining work and submit No 
Objection Certificate from National Wildlife Board.  On 13th August 
2018, this Court stayed the operation and the effect of the notice 
dated 18th/20th July 2018 (Annexure-P).  However, this Court 
directed the petitioners in this group of petitions to attend the 
office of the 5th respondent on 20th August 2018.  This Court 


--- PAGE 14 ---
14 
 
observed that grant of stay will not prevent the concerned 
authorities from taking a decision on the basis of the notice dated 
18th/20th July 2018.  After giving an opportunity of being heard to 
the petitioner, an order was passed on 22nd August 2019 by the 
5th respondent.  By the said order, the 5th respondent suspended 
the quarrying lease until implementation of rehabilitation plan as 
provided in the Rule 8-W of the said Rules of 1994.  It was 
observed that the mining lease shall remain under suspension till 
the compliance of Rule 8-K of the said Rules of 1994 was made.  
It was further observed that as the area of the mining lease was 
coming within the jurisdiction of ten kilometers of Bannerghatta 
National Park, consent of the National Wildlife Board will have to 
be obtained and lastly it was observed that in view of violation of 
Rule 19-A of the said Rules of 1994, the mining lease will remain 
under suspension.  The order of 22nd August 2019 was passed 
subject to outcome of the pending petition.  The order also notes 
that the case of the petitioner will have to be considered in 
accordance with Rule 8-A of the said Rules of 1994.  The said 
order dated 22nd August 2019 (Annexure-R) was challenged by 
filing I.A.No.2/2020.  That is how after the amendment, now the 


--- PAGE 15 ---
15 
 
challenge is confined to the order dated 22nd August 2019 
(Annexure-R). 
 
4. 
The learned counsel for the petitioner has submitted that 
the petitioner has obtained clearance from State Level 
Environmental Clearance Committee and have complied with all 
terms and conditions thereof. He stated that permission for 
blasting has been taken from 6th respondent which has been 
produced.  The learned counsel pointed out that the impugned 
order relies upon only a draft notification of Eco Sensitive Zone of 
Bannerghatta National Park.  The final notification has been 
issued on 11th March 2020 and therefore, Eco Sensitive Zone of 
the Bannerghatta National Park has been considerably reduced.  
The counsel for the petitioners relied upon a decision of this Court 
dated 13th March 2020 in W.P.No.21985/2018.  He also relied 
upon another decision of this Court dated 7th November 2018 in 
W.P.No.49880/2019.  The learned counsel for the petitioner has 
submitted that there is no violation of approved quarrying plan by 
the petitioners and there is no violation of Rule 19-A of the said 
Rules of 1994.  He submitted that now that lease has expired and 
the case of the petitioners for grant of deemed extension under 


--- PAGE 16 ---
16 
 
Rule 8-A of the said Rules of 1994 will have to be considered.  He 
submitted that by setting aside the impugned orders, the case of 
petitioners for grant of deemed extension be considered. 
 
5. 
The 
learned 
Additional 
Government 
Advocate 
has 
submitted that there is a specific allegation of failure to carry out 
quarrying operations as per the approved quarrying plan has 
been made and, therefore, the 5th respondent had power to order 
suspension of quarrying operations by exercising power under 
Sub Rule 2 of Rule 8-K of the said Rules of 1994.  He accepted 
that final notification notifying Eco Sensitive Zone of Bannerghatta 
National Park has been issued.  He pointed out that the allegation 
is also of violation of Rule 19-A of the said Rules of 1994. 
 
6. 
We have carefully considered the submissions.  The writ 
petition was filed for challenging the order dated 8th June 2018 
passed by the 5th respondent of suspending the lease.  However, 
in the notice dated 18th/20th July 2018 (Annexure-P), three 
breaches were set out and the petitioner was directed to submit 
an explanation.  The petitioner was directed to stop the mining 
work immediately till submission of No Objection Certificate from 
National Wildlife Board.  A threat was given to cancel the lease by 


--- PAGE 17 ---
17 
 
exercising the power under Rule 45 of the said Rules of 1994. 
Though the said notice was stayed by this Court, permission was 
granted to the 5th respondent to hear the petitioner and pass 
appropriate order. Thereafter, the last impugned order dated 22nd 
August 2019 (Annexure-R) has been passed.  We may note here 
that 
according 
to 
the 
case 
of 
the 
petitioner 
in 
W.P.No.31590/2018, an application for renewal of lease was 
made on 3rd December 2017.  In fact, that is the case made out in 
all the petitions.  One factual aspect will have to be borne in mind 
that in all these cases, quarrying leases granted to the petitioners 
have expired and applications for renewal of leases are allegedly 
pending.  It is also an admitted fact that the leases were not 
determined or cancelled by the respondents under any of the 
provisions of the said Rules of 1994. The leases are in respect of 
ordinary building stone which is classified as non specified minor 
mineral with effect from 18th July 2018. 
 
7. 
Perusal of the said Rules of 1994  will show that under Sub 
Rule 3 of Rule 6, there is a power to determine the lease on the 
ground that the lessee has committed breach of conditions 
specified in the said Rules of 1994 or in the quarrying 


--- PAGE 18 ---
18 
 
lease/licence. The determination has to be made after giving a 
notice as provided in Sub Rule 3 of Rule 6 of the said Rules of 
1994.  Rule 6 of the said Rules of 1994 does not confer a power 
on any authority to keep a quarrying lease under suspension.  
What is important is wherever the legislature intended, a 
provision has been made for suspension of lease.  For example, 
Rule 8-K of the said Rules of 1994 which provides that if 
quarrying operations are not carried out in accordance with the 
quarrying plan or simplified quarrying plan, Senior Geologist is 
empowered to pass an order of suspension of quarrying 
operations.  Therefore, merely because there is a power to 
determine lease under Sub-Rule (3) of Rule 6, power to suspend 
cannot be read into the said provision. 
   
8. 
As the mining leases subject matter of these petitions were 
subsisting till its expiry, the case of petitioners for grant deemed 
extension under Rule 8-A of the said Rules of 1994 will have to 
be considered in any case as it is claimed that applications for 
renewal made by the petitioners  are pending. 
 
9. 
One of the grounds in impugned Annexure-R is that the 
lease area is situated within the distance of ten kilometres from 


--- PAGE 19 ---
19 
 
Bannerghatta National Park and therefore, it is in Eco Sensitive 
Zone of the said National Park.  However, the final notification 
declaring Eco Sensitive Zone of Bannerghatta national Park has 
been published on 11th March 2020 and therefore, the restrictions 
on mining activities within the distance of ten kilometers from 
Bannerghatta National Park will not apply and now the restrictions 
will remain confined to the declared Eco Sensitive Zone. As 
regards violation of Rule 19-A of the said Rules of 1994, firstly in 
the impugned order, there is no specific finding recorded that the 
petitioners transferred the lease without permission of the 
Competent Authority.  In any case, under Sub Rule 2 of Rule    
19-A of the said Rules of 1994, there is a power vesting on the 
Competent Authority to cancel the lease in the event a transfer of 
lease is effected by the lessee without permission of the 
Competent Authority.  However, as on today there is no 
determination of lease.  The impugned order at Annexure-R also 
purports to be an order passed in exercise of power under Rule 
8-K of the said Rules of 1994.  However, the precise nature of 
breaches allegedly committed by the petitioners of the approved 
quarrying plans have not been set out. In any case, now the 
duration of lease has come to an end.  Therefore, even assuming 


--- PAGE 20 ---
20 
 
that the order of suspension passed in accordance with Rule 8-K 
of the said Rules of 1994 is valid, the same is no more operative.   
 
10. 
Coming back to Eco Sensitive Zone of the Bannerghatta 
National Park, if any part of the lease area is within the radius of 
Eco Sensitive Zone of the Bannerghatta National Park as set out 
in notification dated 11th March 2020, the legal consequences will 
follow.  Thus, on the face of it, the impugned order at Annexure-R 
cannot be sustained and the same will have to  be set aside and 
the respondents will have to be directed to consider the case of 
the petitioners for deemed extension under Rule 8-A of the said 
Rules of 1994.  Annexure-P is only a show cause notice.  After 
Annexure-N, show cause notice at Annexure-P was issued and 
therefore Annexure-N is no more operative. 
 
11. 
The issue whether the petitioners are entitled to deemed 
extension in accordance with Rule 8-A of the said Rules of 1994 
is kept open to be decided by the concerned authority.  The issue 
whether the quarrying area of the petitioners falls in the Eco 
Sensitive Zone of the said National Park is also kept open.    
 
12. 
Accordingly, we pass the following: 


--- PAGE 21 ---
21 
 
ORDER 
(i) 
The impugned orders dated 22nd August 2020 
at Annexure-R in all the petitions are hereby 
set-aside.  The other impugned orders/notices 
are inoperative; 
 
 
(ii) 
We direct the 5th respondent to examine 
whether applications for renewal were made 
by the petitioners in these petitions in 
accordance with law. If the applications for 
renewal have been made in accordance with 
law and the same are pending, the cases of 
the petitioners shall be examined by the 
concerned respondents for grant of a deemed 
extension under Rule 8-A of the said Rules of 
1994.  Appropriate decision shall be taken 
within a period of two months from today; 
 
(iii) 
We clarify that the cases for deemed 
extension shall be considered without being 
influenced by the impugned notices/impugned 
orders;

22 
 
(iv) 
The petitions are partly allowed on the above 
terms with no order as to costs.
