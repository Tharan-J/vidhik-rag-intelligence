ORDER
1. 
Heard the learned counsel appearing for the petitioners.  
The breach alleged in these petitions is of following part of the 
order dated 25th March 2019 passed by the learned Single Judge, 
which reads thus:   
"So far as Writ Petition in Nos.42944/2018 
and 44747-44782/2018 are concerned, this Court 
would direct the concerned authorities to consider 
the representation of petitioners to implement the 
grant order dated 22.12.2012 if the same is in 
accordance with law, at the earliest. 
With this, the order dated 27.02.2019 
passed in this writ petition is clarified by allowing 
application in I.A.No.1/2019." 
2. 
The representation of the petitioners ought to have been 
considered as directed at the earliest.  Therefore, the 
representation ought to have been decided within a reasonable 
time.  Annexure-E are the copies of the representations made by 
the petitioners in July 2019.  On 27th January 2021, the present 
contempt petitions have been filed. 
3. 
On 2nd February 2021, the State of Karnataka was ordered 
to be impleaded as a pro forma respondent.  On 10th March 2021, 
a contention was raised before this Court that the case will have 
to be placed before the Akrama-Sakrama Committee.  This Court 


--- PAGE 32 ---
 
32 
rejected the said contention.  On 7th April 2021, this Court passed 
an order that if the compliance was not reported till the next date, 
it will be a case of aggravated contempt.   
4. 
Today, the learned Additional Government Advocate has 
placed on record a copy of the endorsement dated 12th April 2021.  
The said endorsement records a conclusion that there is no grant 
order made in favour of the petitioners on 22nd December 2012.   
5. 
The learned counsel appearing for the complainants 
submits that a frivolous stand has been taken in the said 
endorsement dated 12th April 2021 that there is no grant order 
dated 22nd December 2012.  He submitted that the said stand is 
contrary to the record filed in the Writ Petitions.  He would, 
therefore, submit that the said endorsement will not constitute 
compliance with the order dated 25th March 2019.   
6. 
We have quoted the order of the breach which is alleged.  
The direction of the Court was to consider the representation of 
the writ petitioners to implement the grant order dated 22nd
December 2012, "if the same is in accordance with law".  Now the 
stand taken in the aforesaid endorsement is that such a grant 
order does not exist.  Therefore, it is not possible to accept the 


--- PAGE 33 ---
 
33 
submission that there is no compliance made with the direction of 
which breach is alleged.  The petitioners can always challenge the 
said endorsement in accordance with law. 
7. 
As noted earlier, the order dated 25th March 2019 ought to 
have been complied a within reasonable time.  Excuse of Covid-
19 is also not available as Covid-19 becomes relevant only from 
March 2020.  Even after filing of this petition and grant of 
adjournments, the endorsement has been issued on 12th April 
2021.  Thus, the State Government has taken time of about 
twenty-five months for compliance of the order of which breach is 
alleged.  It is impossible to accept that the endorsement has been 
issued within a reasonable time.  It is only because of the gross 
delay that the petitioners were driven to the Court by filing these 
petitions.  Therefore, the State Government will have to be 
saddled with costs quantified at Rs.5,000/- in each petition.   
8. 
Accordingly, we pass the following order: 
(a) 
we dispose of the petitions in view of belated 
compliance;
