16. 
Section 139 mandates that, unless contrary is 
proved, it is to be presumed that the holder of a cheque 
received the cheque of the nature referred to in Section 


--- PAGE 14 ---
Crl.A. No.114/2011

138 of N.I. Act for the discharge, in whole or in part, of any 
debt or other liability.  It was further observed in the same 
judgment that a meaningful reading of the provisions of the 
Negotiable Instruments Act including, in particular, 
Sections 20, 87 and 139, makes it amply clear that the 
person who signs a cheque and makes it over to the 
payee remains liable unless he adduces evidence to 
rebut the presumption that the cheque had been issued 
for payment of a debt or in discharge of a liability. 
However, the Hon’ble Apex Court has further 
observed in the same judgment that the presumption is 
rebuttable by proving to the contrary.   
Therefore, from the above judgment in Bir Singh’s 
case (supra), it can be noticed that Section 139 of NI 
Act acts as an exception to the general rule as to the 
burden of proof and shifts the onus on the accused to 
prove by cogent evidence that there was no debt or 
liability.  At the same time, it is also to be borne in mind 


--- PAGE 15 ---
Crl.A. No.114/2011 
 
                      
15      
that mere denial or rebuttal by the accused, itself, is not 
sufficient, as observed by their Lordship in  Bir Singh’s 
case (supra).

said presumption.
