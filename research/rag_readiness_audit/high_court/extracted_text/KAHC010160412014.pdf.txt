ORAL JUDGMENT 
 
The complainant/respondent in Crl.A.No.83/2012 on the 
file of the learned Principal District and Sessions Judge, 
Davanagere (for short 'the First Appellate Court') is impugning 
the judgment dated 03.03.2014 acquitting accused No.1 for the 
offence punishable under Section 498A of Indian Penal Code 
(for short 'the IPC) and Sections 3 and 4 of Dowry Prohibition 
Act (for short 'the DP Act') by setting aside the judgment of 
conviction and order of sentence dated 30.06.2012 passed in 
CC No.290/2007 on the file of learned JMFC, Honnali (for short 
'the Trial Court').  
 
2. 
For the sake of convenience, the parties shall be 
referred to as per their rank and status before the Trial Court.

3. 
Brief facts of the case are that,  the complainant 
filed the first information against accused Nos.1 to 3 alleging 
commission of offence punishable under Section 498A of IPC 
and under Sections 3 and 4 of DP act. It is the specific 
contention of the complainant that she had married accused 
No.1 on 04.07.2002 and at the time of marriage, the accused 
have demanded dowry of Rs.1,20,000/- out of which, they 
have received Rs.1,14,000/- on various dates. It is her further 
contention that after marriage, she was ill-treated by the 
accused by demanding additional dowry. It is the specific 
contention of the complainant that on 13th and 14th of February, 
2007, accused No.1 assaulted her all over the body, caused 
injuries and treated her with cruelty. There by it is stated that 
accused Nos.1 to 3 have committed offence as stated above.  
 
4. 
On the basis of statement of the complainant 
recorded, while she was taking treatment at McGann Hospital, 
Shivamogga, the FIR came to be registered against accused 
Nos.1 to 3 for the above stated offences and after 
investigation, the charge sheet came to be filed. The 
complainant examined herself as PW1 and got examined PWs.2 
to 20 and got marked Exs.P.1 to 15 and identified MO1 in

support of her contention. The accused have denied all the 
incriminating materials available on record and got examined 
DWs.1 to 5 and got marked Exs.D.1 to 11 in support of their 
contention. The trial Court after taking into consideration all 
these materials on record, came to the conclusion that the 
prosecution is successful in proving the guilt of accused No.1 
for the offence punishable under Section 498A of IPC and 
sentenced him to undergo simple imprisonment for 3 years and 
to pay fine of Rs.2,000/- and in default to undergo SI for 1 
month and he was also convicted for the offence punishable 
under Section 3 of DP Act and was sentenced to undergo SI for 
5 years and to pay fine of Rs.1,14,000/- and in default to 
undergo SI for 3 months. He was convicted for the offence 
punishable under Section 4 of DP Act and sentenced to undergo 
SI for 1 year and to pay fine of Rs.2,000/- and in default, to 
undergo SI for 1 month. It is also ordered that out of fine 
amount collected by accused No.1, a sum of Rs.1,14,000/- to 
be paid to PW1 as compensation. However, the trial Court 
acquitted accused Nos.2 and 3 for the above said offences. 
Being aggrieved by the same, accused No.1 challenged his 
conviction before the first Appellate Court in Crl.A.No.83/2012.

The first appellate Court on re-appreciation of materials on

successful in proving the guilt of the accused and accordingly, 
allowed the appeal, set-aside the impugned judgment of 
conviction, convicting the accused No.1. Being aggrieved by the 
same, the complainant is before this Court.

6. 
In view of the rival contentions urged by the 
learned counsel for both the parties, the point that would arise 
for my consideration is: 
“Whether the impugned judgment of 
acquittal passed by the First Appellate 
Court suffers from perversity or illegality 
and calls for interference by this court? "

My answer to the above point is in ‘Affirmative' for the 
following: 
R E A S O N S 
 
7. 
It is the specific contention of the prosecution that 
PW1 being complainant married accused No.1 on 04.07.2002 
and this fact contention is not in dispute. It is the contention of 
the prosecution that accused No.1 had demanded dowry of 
Rs.1,20,000/- at the time of marriage and out of the same, 
Rs.1,14,000/- was paid by the complainant and her parents. To 
prove this contention, the complainant examined herself as 
PW1, her father as PW4, brother as PW5, an independent 
witnesses PW9 and PW13 and her mother as PW10. All these 
witnesses consistently stated about the demand made by 
accused No.1 for dowry and receiving an amount of 
Rs.1,14,000/- on various dates, as stated by them. Even 
though, these witnesses were cross examined at length, 
nothing has been elicited to disbelieve their version. Evidence 
of these prosecution witnesses are consistent with regard to 
material particulars. Minor inconsistencies in the evidence is 
liable to be ignored as they do not go to the root of the matter.

There is absolutely no reason as to disbelieve the version of  
the independent witnesses who supported the case of the 
prosecution. It is pertinent to note PWs.11 and 12 have also 
spoken about the demand for dowry and PW12 is the relative of 
the accused. Even though accused No.1 had stepped into the 
witness box and deposed as DW1, the version of prosecution 
regarding demand and acceptance of dowry remain unshaken.  
Therefore, I am of the opinion that the prosecution is successful 
in proving the guilt of accused No.1 for the offence punishable 
under Sections 3 and 4 of DP Act who is liable for conviction.  
 
8. 
Second contention taken by the prosecution is with 
regard to treating of PW1 with cruelty. PW1 has stepped into 
the witness box and deposed regarding ill-treatment meted by  
accused in general and by accused No.1 in particular. She 
states about the incident that had taken place on 13.02.2007 
and 14.02.2007 and narrated in detail about the physical 
torture meted to her. Her contention was also reiterated by  her 
father, mother and brother and also the other witnesses. It is 
pertinent to note that the statement of PW1 was recorded while 
she was admitted in McGann Hospital, on 15.02.2007. The 
Doctor, who examined PW1 has issued the wound certificate as

per Ex.P11, which discloses that she had sustained as many as 
7 injuries, which are all simple in nature. However, the wound 
certificates fully corroborates the contention of PW1 that she 
was physically assaulted and tortured by accused No.1. 
 
9. 
The author of Ex.P.11 i.e., the Doctor working in 
McGann Hospital was examined as PW17. Even though the 
learned counsel for the accused cross-examined the witness at 
length, nothing has been elicited from him to disbelieve his 
version. As per Ex.P.11 issued by PW17, the history of injury as 
stated by the injured is that the incident had occurred from 
08.02.2007 up to a week and the injured was examined on 
15.02.2007 at 5.00 p.m. It is the specific contention of PW1 
that she was assaulted by accused No.1 on 13.02.2007 and 
14.02.2007, which is probabalised by Ex.P.11 and evidence of 
PW17. PW1 and other prosecution witnesses also spoke about 
the cruelty meted to PW1 on many other occasions. There is 
absolutely no reason to disbelieve the version of the injured 
PW1 and PW17-the doctor who treated her on  15.02.2007.  
 
10. 
It is pertinent to note that Ex.P.15 is the copy of the 
birth certificate of child on 14.09.2010 and father being

12. 
I have gone through the judgment of conviction 
passed by the trial Court, it has taken into consideration the 
oral and documentary evidence placed before it and had arrived

No.1 and in acquitting other accused. 
 
13. 
I have also gone through impugned judgment of 
acquittal passed by the First Appellate Court. Even though the

First Appellate Court referred to evidence of various witnesses, 
it has given flimsy reasons to disbelieve the version of the 
prosecution which cannot be sustained when there are strong 
materials such as wound certificate, birth certificate of the 
child, said to have born to accused No.1 through his second 
marriage, which is coupled with evidence of the injured-PW1 
and corroborated by the other prosecution witnesses. I do not 
find any justification for First Appellate Court to ignore the 
clinching materials for the purpose of acquitting the accused. 
The First Appellate Court has given unreasonable weightage  to 
the minor inconsistencies, which resulted in grave injustice.  
 
14. 
Learned counsel for the appellant contended that 
even though learned Magistrate has passed an order for grant 
of maintenance, accused No.1 is successfully denying the 
payment of maintenance amount and there by the complainant 
and her daughter are virtually in hard situation and they are at 
mercy of others. Considering the facts and circumstances of the 
case in the light of the matter is placed on record, the conduct 
of accused No.1 appears to be not acceptable and is liable for 
conviction. Hence, I am of the opinion that the impugned 
judgment of acquittal passed by the First Appellate Court is
