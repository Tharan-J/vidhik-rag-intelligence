18. 
In the decision reported in 2009 ACJ 287 
(National Insurance Company Vs. Smt. Pushpa Rana 
and others), The Hon'ble Delhi High Court was pleased 
to hold that, the certified copies of criminal court such as 
FIR, recovery memo and mechanical inspection of vehicle 
are documents of sufficient proof to reach the conclusion 
that, the driver was negligent - proceedings under Motor 
Vehicle Act are not akin to proceedings in a civil suit. 
Hence, strict rules of evidence are not required to be 
followed in this regard. 
 
19. 
In a petition for compensation under section 166 of 
Motor vehicles Act, the claimants have to prove the 
incident only on preponderance of probabilities and the 
standard of proof beyond reasonable doubt is not 
required as held by the Hon’ble Apex Court in the 
decision reported in 2011 SAR (CIVIL) 319 in the case 
of Kusum and others V/s Satbir and others. The ratio 
followed in the said case as held in the case of Bimla 
Devi and Others V/s. Himachal Road Transport 
Corporation and Others (2009)13 SCC 530, wherein 
it is held that, it was necessary to be borne in mind that 
strict proof of an accident caused by a particular bus in a 
particular manner may not be possible to be done by the 
claimants. The claimants were merely to establish their 
case on the touchstone of preponderance of probability. 
The standard of proof beyond reasonable doubt could not 
have been applied.
