THIS PETITION, COMING ON FOR ORDERS, THIS DAY, 
THE COURT MADE THE FOLLOWING: 
 
ORDER 
 
The petitioner-accused No.5 is before this Court 
seeking grant of bail under Section 439 of Cr.P.C., in Crime 
No. 101/2024 of Bengaluru CEN Crime Police Station, 
pending in on the file of the learned Chief Judicial Magistrate 
(CJM), Bengaluru Rural District, Bengaluru registered for the 
offences punishable under Sections 66 (C) and 66 (D) of 
Information Technology Act and Section 419, 420 of Indian 
Penal Code (for short 'IPC') on the basis of the first 
information lodged by the informant-  Sri.Santhosh Kumar.  
 
2. 
Heard Sri. Ram Singh K, learned counsel for the 
petitioner and Smt. K.P. Yashodha, learned HCGP for 
respondent –State.  Perused the materials on record.  
 
3. 
In view of the rival contentions urged by the 
learned counsel for both the parties, the point that would 
arise for my consideration is: 
“Whether the petitioner is entitled 
for grant of bail under Section 439 of 
Cr.P.C.?”

My answer to the above point is in ‘Affirmative’ for the 
following: 
R E A S O N S 
 
4. 
The petitioner – accused No.5 is seeking grant of 
bail.  He was apprehended on 04.04.2024, since then he is in 
judicial custody. The complainant lodged the first information 
about cheating and criminal breach of trust.  Accused Nos.1 
to 6 were apprehended.  It is stated that accused Nos. 1 and 
4 were released on default bail.  Accused Nos.3 and 6 were 
granted bail.   
5.  It is the contention of learned counsel for the 
petitioner that the petitioner is also entitled for parity and to 
be released on bail.  However, it is the contention of learned 
High Court Government Pleader that the allegations made 
against the petitioner and the allegation made against 
accused Nos.3 and 6 are different.  Moreover, accused Nos.1 
and 4 were released on default bail.  Therefore, petitioner is 
not entitled for the benefit of parity.

6. 
The investigation is already completed and the 
charge sheet is filed.  It is stated that further investigation 
under Section 173(8) of Cr.P.C is undertaken and since it is a 
cyber crime some more details are required to be secured.  
In the charge sheet serious allegations are made against the 
present petitioner stating that he collected the amount from 
various customers and an amount of Rs.3,51,245,078/-  was 
received by him. It is stated that the said  amount was 
received in the account of accused No.4.  It is the present 
petitioner through his mobile phone has transferred the 
amount to various other accounts and with the help of 
accused No.6 transferred the amount to the person in 
Malaysia. The charge sheet further discloses that the Bank 
details transaction held by accused No.5 is yet to be 
collected.  However, the fact remains that accused Nos.1, 3, 
4 and 6 are already enlarged on bail and even though 
accused Nos.1 and 4 were released on default bail, the 
prosecution has not sought for cancellation of bail  after filing 
of the charge sheet.  The investigating officer will be at 
liberty to take the petitioner to custody with the permission

of jurisdictional Magistrate.  Hence, I am of the opinion that 
at this stage the petitioner is entitled for grant of bail on

7. 
In view of the above, I answer the above point in 
the affirmative and proceed to pass the following: 
 
ORDER 
 
 The petition is allowed.   
The petitioner is ordered to be enlarged on bail in 
Crime No. 101/2024 of Bengaluru CEN Crime Police Station, 
on obtaining the bond in a sum of Rs.2,00,000/- (Rupees 
Two Lakhs only) with two sureties for the likesum to the 
satisfaction of the jurisdictional Court, subject to the 
following conditions: 
a). 
The petitioner shall not commit similar 
offences.  
b). 
The petitioner shall not threaten or tamper 
with the prosecution witnesses. 
c). 
The petitioner shall appear before the Court 
as and when required.
