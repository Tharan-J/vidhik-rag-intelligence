JUDGMENT 
 
This appeal is filed under Section 173(1) of the 
Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (‘MV Act’ for short), by the 
claimants challenging the judgment and award in MVC 
No.131/2014 on the file of the Senior Civil Judge and 
JMFC and Addl.MACT, Hiriyur dated 01.04.2017. 
 
 
2. 
For the sake of convenience, the parties 
herein are referred as per the ranks occupied by them 
before the Tribunal. 
 
3. 
There is no serious dispute regarding factual 
aspects. The brief factual matrix leading to the case 


--- PAGE 3 ---
 
 
3
are that on 17.02.2013 at about 4.00 p.m. the 
husband of claimant No.1 Pandurangaiah was riding 
Hero Honda motor cycle bearing registration No.KA-44-
K-5894 from Hiriyur to his village. At that time, the 
driver of Eicher lorry bearing registration No.TN-40-F-
0694 drove it in a rash and negligent manner and 
dashed 
against 
the 
motor 
cycle. 
As 
a 
result 
Pandurangaiah fell down and suffered grievous injuries 
and succumbed due to the said injuries. It is also 
alleged that he was a sole bread earner in the family 
and was earning Rs.20,000/- p.m. from agricultural 
and sericulture. Hence, claimants filed the claim 
petition under Section 166 of the MV Act claiming 
compensation of Rs.25,00,000/- with interest thereon. 
 
 
4. 
Respondents 
appeared 
and 
filed 
their 
objection statement. The respondent No.2 denied that 
accident is because of the actionable negligence on the 
part of the driver of the offending lorry. But 
alternatively he contended that the vehicle was duly 
insured with respondent No.1 and the driver was

part of the deceased himself. Further he has also 
contended that there is breach of policy conditions as 
driver of the offending vehicle was not possessing valid 
and effective driving license and hence, disputed the 
claim. 
 
 
 5. 
The Tribunal after assessing the oral as well 
as documentary evidence has come to a conclusion 
that claimants are entitled for a total compensation of 
Rs.19,80,000/- with interest at the rate of 7.5 % p.a. 
However, it has fastened the liability on respondent 
No.2-owner of the offending vehicle on the ground that 
there is breach of policy conditions. 
 
 
6. 
Being aggrieved by this judgment and 
award, the claimants are before this Court seeking

7. 
Heard the arguments advanced by the 
learned counsel for the appellants-claimants and 
learned counsel for respondent-insurer. Perused the 
records. 
 
 
8. 
Learned 
counsel 
for 
the 
appellants-
claimants would contend that there is no serious 
dispute of the fact that the deceased Pandurangaiah 
met with an accident on 17.02.2013 at 4.00 p.m. and 
succumbed because of the injuries sustained by him. 
The Tribunal has held that the accident in question is 
because of the actionable negligence on the part of the 
driver of the offending lorry. This finding is not 
challenged by the respondents No.1 and 2. However, 
the Tribunal has fastened the liability on respondent 
No.2 alone by exonerating respondent No.1 on the 
ground that driver was not possessing valid and 
effective driving license which is improper as deceased 
was a third party and also sought for enhancement. 


--- PAGE 6 ---
 
 
6
9. 
Per contra, learned counsel for respondent-
Insurance company would contend that the deceased 
was not possessing valid driving license and the driving 
license produced by him disclose that it was pertaining 
to LMV transport but the vehicle involved is not a LMV 
as gross weight of the vehicle is more than 8,000. 
Alternatively he would contend that in case of 
fastening the liability on insurance company the order 
regarding pay and recovery needs to be passed. He 
has also contended that interest awarded is on higher 
side and the compensation requires to be recalculated 
as age was taken on lower side. 
 
10. Having heard the arguments and perusing 
the records, there is no dispute of the fact regarding 
the death of Pandurangaiah in the road traffic accident. 
Though the Tribunal has taken his age as 40 based on 
LIC bond at Ex.P29, however, Ex.P30 ration card  and 
Ex.P2 post mortem report disclose that age of the 
deceased was 45 years. Apart from that Pw.1 has also 
specifically asserted in her evidence that deceased was 


--- PAGE 7 ---
 
 
7
aged about 45 years. Under such circumstances, only 
basing on LIC bond, the Tribunal has erred by taking 
the age as 40 years by ignoring the other material 
evidence including evidence of Pw.1. Considering these 
documents, the age is required to be taken at 45 years 
as against 40 years. The Tribunal has taken the income 
of the deceased at Rs.10,000/- p.m. Admittedly the 
accident has occurred in the year 2013 and this Court 
is consistently taking the notional income at Rs.8,000/- 
p.m. in respect of the accidents occurred in the year 
2013. However, in the instant case, the records 
disclose that the claimants also produced 36 coconut 
bills at Ex.P27, sale bills at Ex.P28, auction chits at 
Ex.P24 to 26. These documents disclose that he is 
having 
agricultural 
income 
also. 
Under 
these 
circumstances, the Tribunal after assessing these 
aspects has taken the income at Rs.10,000/- p.m. 
which appears to be reasonable one. 
 
 
11. If the age of the deceased is taken as 45 
years, then as per the decision in the case of National 


--- PAGE 8 ---
 
 
8
Insurance Company Limited Vs. Pranay Sethi and 
Others  reported in (2017) 16 SCC 680, 25% of the 
future prospects are required to be added to the 
income of the deceased and in that event his monthly 
income would be Rs.12,500/- p.m. Considering the age 
of the deceased, multiplier 14 is applicable. Since there 
are 4 dependents 1/4th is required to be deducted 
towards personal expenses of the deceased. Hence, 
the loss of dependency would work out as under: 
 
Rs.12,500/- X 12 X 14 X 3/4 = Rs.15,75,000/- 
 
 
12. There are 4 dependents who are widow and 
children of the deceased and as per the decisions in 
the cases of Magma General Insurance Company 
Limited vs. Nanu Ram Alias Chuhru Ram and 
others reported in (2018) 18 SCC 130 and Satinder 
Kaur @ Satwinder Kaur & Ors. vs. United India 
Insurance Co. Ltd. reported in AIR 2020 SC 3076, 
each of them are entitled for consortium to the extent 
of Rs.40,000/-. However, in view of the recent decision 
of the Hon’ble Apex Court in the case of  Rasmita

Rs.44,000/- and as such under the head of loss of 
consortium 
claimants 
would 
be 
entitled 
for 
Rs.1,76,000/-. Further under the head of loss of estate 
they are entitled for Rs.16,500/- and Rs.15,000/- 
under the head of funeral expenses. Hence, the 
claimants would be entitled for a fair and just

13. Hence, the claimants would be entitled for a

14. At the same time the Tribunal has awarded 
interest at the rate of 7.5% p.a. and considering the 
prevailing fixed deposit rates, this Court is consistently 
awarding interest at the rate of 6% p.a. 
 
 
15. The owner on whom the liability is fastened 
has not challenged the award. The evidence on record 
disclose that the driver of the offending lorry was 
possessing driving license for LMV transport. But Ex.R3 
RCB extract of the lorry disclose that the gross weight 
of the vehicle is more that 8,720. Hence, as per the 
definition of Section 2(21) of the MV Act the vehicle 
does not fall within the category of LMV and as such 
the license Ex.R2 which is pertaining to LMV cannot be 
used for driving the offending vehicle. Hence, there is 
clear breach of policy conditions. However, this issue 


--- PAGE 11 ---
 
 
11
has  been now covered by the decision in the case of 
Pappu Deo Yadav Vs. Naresh Kumar & Ors. 
reported in  AIR 2020 SC 4424. Admittedly the 
deceased was a third party and as such the insurance 
company is liable to pay the compensation with liberty 
to recover the same from the owner-respondent No.2 
as there is no serious dispute regarding coverage of 
the insurance by respondent No.2. Though the owner 
has not challenged the award as well as liability, now 
the liability  has been fastened on insurance company 
which requires to be reduced and on recalculation the 
quantum also requires to be reduced. Though the 
insurance company has not challenged, it is the duty of 
the Court to award a just compensation and hence, the 
award requires to be reduced properly as observed 
above. However, the appeal needs to be allowed in 
part insofar as it relates to liability and accordingly, I 
proceed to pass the following: 
ORDER
