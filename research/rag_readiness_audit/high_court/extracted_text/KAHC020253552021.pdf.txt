dated 20.09.2021 in Original Suit No.1/2019 on the file of the 
Principal Senior Civil Judge, Karwar, (for short, hereinafter 
referred to as ‘Trial Court’),allowing I.A.No.6 filed by defendant 
Nos.1 and 2 under Order VII Rule 11(a) and (d) read with

3. 
It is the case of the plaintiff that, plaintiff and

10. 
The plaintiff has valued the suit under Section 35(2) 
of the Act, 1958.  Undoubtedly, the suit schedule properties 
have been ancestral properties and therefore, even though the 
plaintiff cannot seeks relief of cancellation of the sale deed, in 
which the defendants have alienated the property as per 
registered sale deed dated 16.11.2012, however being a co-
parcener in the joint family constituting the plaintiff and 
defendants, the plaintiff is entitled for share if she proves the 
same before the Trial Court. It is well established principle of 
law that, in a suit for partition the parties are having pre-
existing right in the suit schedule properties and as the 
partition is not a transfer by itself and it does not convey 
nothing to the parties and therefore, the finding recorded by 
the Trial Court cannot be accepted compelling the plaintiff to 
pay the Court fee in respect of the sale deed said to have been 
executed by defendant Nos.1 and 2 as stated in I.A. No.6.  That 
apart, plaintiff is not a party to the registered sale deed dated 
16.11.2012 and therefore, not being a party to the said 
registered sale deed, plaintiff need not seek cancellation of said 
registered sale deed, however the plaintiff seeks relief of 
partition and separate possession in respect of the suit

schedule properties or declaration as such.  The said principle 
was enunciated by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of 
Suhrid Singh alias Sardool Singh Vs. Randhir Singh and 
Others reported (2010) 12 SCC 112, wherein it is held that 
only the executants to the deed is to seek relief of annulment 
of said deed and sought for annulment of the said deed in 
question.  A non-executant to the deed can claim only relief of 
declaration as the said deed is void.  Paragraph No.7 of the said 
judgment reads as under: 
7. Where the executant of a deed wants it 
to be annulled, he has to seek cancellation of the 
deed.  But if a non-executant seeks annulment of 
a deed, he has to seek a declaration that the 
deed is invalid, or non est, or illegal or that it is 
not binding on him.  The difference between a 
prayer for cancellation and declaration in regard 
to a deed of transfer/conveyance, can be brought 
out by the following illustration relating to A and 
B, two brothers.  A executes a sale deed in favour 
of C. Subsequently A wants to avoid the sale.  A 
has to sue for cancellation of the deed.  On the 
other hand, if B, who is not the executants of the 
deed, wants to avoid it, he has to sue for a 
declaration that the deed executed by A is 
invalid/void and non est/illegal and he is not 
bound by it.  In essence both may be suing to

have the deed set aside or declared as non-
binding.  But the form is different and court fee is 
also different.  If A, the executants of the deed, 
seeks cancellation sated in the sale deed.  If B, 
who is a or void and does not bind him or his 
share, he has to merely pay a fixed court fee of 
Rs.19.50 under Article 17(iii) of the Second 
Schedule of the Act. But if B, a non-executant, is 
not in possession, and he seeks not only a 
declaration that the sale deed is invalid, but also 
the consequential relief of possession, he has to 
pay an ad valorem court fee as provided under 
Section 7(iv)(c) of the Act.”   
 
11. 
Following the declaration made by the Hon’ble 
Supreme Case in the case of Suhrid Singh (supra), the Trial 
Court ought not to have rejected the plaint by passing the 
impugned order.  Accordingly, we find force in the submission 
made by the learned counsel appearing for the appellant/s as 
the matter requires reconsideration by the Trial Court. Hence, 
the points for determination referred to above favours the 
plaintiff.  In the result, we pass the following: 
O R D E R 
1) 
Regular First Appeal is allowed.
